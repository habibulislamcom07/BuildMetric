The Ultimate Guide to Paint and Wallpaper Estimation

Whether you are painting a single accent wall or wallpapering an entire room, precise estimation saves time and money. Paint is generally calculated at 350 square feet per gallon, while wallpaper requires accounting for pattern repeats. Our calculators simplify this by handling the complex wall area math, subtracting doors and windows, and providing exact material quantities. Always add 10-15% for waste.

Paint Coverage: The Math Behind the Gallons

Paint coverage is not an exact science. It depends on the porosity of the surface, the color you are applying, and the quality of the paint. However, the industry standard is that 1 gallon of paint covers roughly 350 square feet in a single coat.

In the UK and Australia, paint is sold in liters. The standard coverage is roughly 12 to 14 square meters per liter for a single coat. Our Paint Coverage Calculator allows you to toggle between US gallons and metric liters.

Why Do You Need Two Coats?

A single coat of paint is rarely enough. The first coat is absorbed by the porous surface (drywall, wood, or masonry). The second coat provides the actual color, durability, and washability.

If you are painting a dark color over a light color (or vice versa), you may need a tinted primer plus two topcoats. Our tool automatically calculates for two coats, but you can adjust the coat count for your specific project.

Understanding Sheen Levels

Paint is not just about color; it is also about finish. The "sheen" determines how much light reflects off the surface and how easy it is to clean. Choosing the wrong sheen can ruin the look of a room.

  • Flat / Matte: No reflection. Hides imperfections well. Ideal for ceilings, low-traffic areas, and adult bedrooms. Difficult to clean.
  • Eggshell: A subtle, soft sheen. The most popular choice for living rooms and hallways. Offers a good balance of durability and aesthetic.
  • Satin: A pearl-like finish. Highly durable and washable. Ideal for kitchens, bathrooms, and high-traffic hallways.
  • Semi-Gloss / Gloss: High reflection. Very durable and easy to clean. Best for trim, doors, cabinets, and high-moisture areas. Highlights surface imperfections.

For exterior paint, satin or semi-gloss is often used to resist moisture and UV rays. For interior trim, semi-gloss is the standard choice.

The Critical Role of Primer

Primer is often skipped by DIYers, which is a costly mistake. Primer is a preparatory coating that seals the surface and provides a strong bond for the topcoat.

You must use a primer if:

  • You are painting new drywall (which is highly porous).
  • You are painting over a dark color with a light paint.
  • You are painting over glossy surfaces (which need sanding and a bonding primer).
  • You are painting over stained wood or water-damaged areas (use a stain-blocking primer).

For new drywall, use a PVA primer. For walls with stains or smoke damage, use a shellac-based primer (like BIN). For exterior wood, use an oil-based or water-based wood primer.

Global Paint Standards: US vs. UK vs. Australia

While paint is universal, the measurements, terminology, and VOC regulations vary significantly.

USA: Paint is sold in gallons (1 gal = 3.78L). VOC regulations vary by state (California has the strictest limits). Behr, Sherwin-Williams, and Benjamin Moore are the leading brands.

UK: Paint is sold in liters (2.5L, 5L, 10L). All paints sold in the UK must comply with EU VOC limits. Dulux and Crown are the dominant brands.

Australia: Paint is sold in liters. The APAS (Australian Paint Approval Scheme) certifies low-VOC and environmentally friendly paints. Taubmans and Wattyl are popular brands.

Wallpaper: The Math of Pattern Matching

Unlike paint, wallpaper calculations are complicated by the pattern repeat. The pattern repeat is the distance before the design repeats itself on the roll.

If you are using a wallpaper with a large repeat (e.g., 24 inches), you will have to cut off significant waste at the top and bottom of each strip to align the design. This can increase the number of rolls needed by 15% to 25%.

Our Wallpaper Calculator automatically accounts for the pattern repeat. You simply select your pattern size from the dropdown, and the tool calculates the extra rolls required.

Straight Match vs. Drop Match

There are two main types of pattern matching:

  • Straight Match: The pattern aligns horizontally across the seams at the same height. It is simpler to install and wastes less paper.
  • Drop Match: The pattern on the next strip drops down by half the repeat to align. This is common with large floral or damask patterns and requires more waste.

Epoxy Floors: A Chemical Reaction, Not a Paint

Epoxy is not paint. It is a two-part thermosetting polymer made from resin and hardener. When mixed, a chemical reaction occurs that creates a rigid, durable plastic.

Epoxy is highly sensitive to thickness. If applied too thin, it will peel. If applied too thick, it can trap air and bubble. Our Epoxy Floor Calculator allows you to specify your desired thickness (in mils or microns) and calculates the exact volume of resin and hardener required.

Pro Tip: Epoxy will not bond to a smooth, sealed, or damp concrete surface. You must mechanically profile the concrete (diamond grinding or shot blasting) to achieve a CSP 3-4 profile before applying epoxy.

Surface Preparation: The Invisible 80%

Whether you are painting or wallpapering, the finish will only be as good as the surface underneath.

Before painting, wash the walls with a degreaser and fill any holes with lightweight spackle. For wallpaper, the walls must be perfectly smooth and sealed with a primer (sizing) to allow the paper to slide into place and to make future removal easier.

If the drywall is damaged, you may need to replace it before painting. Use our Drywall Calculator for exact sheet counts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Paint & Wallpaper FAQs

Direct answers to common questions about paint coverage, wallpaper, and epoxy floors.

01

How much paint do I need for a room?
Calculate the wall area (perimeter × height), subtract doors and windows, and divide by the paint's coverage (usually 350 sq ft per gallon). Multiply by 2 for two coats. Our Paint Coverage Calculator does this instantly.
02

How many rolls of wallpaper do I need?
Measure the wall area, subtract openings, and divide by the wallpaper roll's coverage. Always add 15-20% for pattern matching (the pattern repeat). Use our Wallpaper Calculator for exact rolls.
03

What is the difference between primer and paint?
Primer is a preparatory coating that seals porous surfaces, blocks stains, and helps the topcoat adhere. Paint is the decorative and protective topcoat. You should almost always prime new drywall, bare wood, or dark walls before painting.
04

How many coats of paint should I apply?
Two coats of high-quality paint are standard for a durable, even finish. If you are painting a dark color over a light color (or vice versa), you may need a tinted primer plus two topcoats for full coverage.
05

What is the best sheen for a bathroom?
Satin or semi-gloss sheens are best for bathrooms and kitchens because they are moisture-resistant and easy to clean. Flat or matte finishes should be avoided in high-humidity areas as they can grow mold.
06

How long does paint take to dry?
Most water-based (latex) paints are dry to the touch in 1 hour and can be recoated in 2-4 hours. Oil-based paints take 6-8 hours to dry and 24 hours before recoating. Always check the can for exact instructions.
07

Can I paint over wallpaper?
It is not recommended. The moisture from the paint can cause the wallpaper to bubble, peel, or release from the wall. It is always best to remove old wallpaper, clean the wall, and apply a fresh coat of primer before painting.
