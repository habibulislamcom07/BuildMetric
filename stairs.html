The Ultimate Guide to Stair Estimation

Stairs are the most math-intensive part of residential construction. A mistake in the rise or run can make the staircase dangerous or fail inspection. Our calculators help you determine the exact stringer length, rise, run, and baluster spacing. Key Rule: Always verify your calculations against local building codes (IRC, BS, or NCC) before cutting any wood.

The Anatomy of a Safe Staircase

A staircase is a complex system of interacting parts. Understanding these components is critical for any builder.

1. Rise and Run

The rise is the vertical distance between the top of one tread and the top of the next. The run (or going) is the horizontal depth of the tread. The relationship between rise and run determines the steepness and comfort of the stairs.

In the US, the IRC allows a maximum rise of 7.75 inches and a minimum run of 10 inches. In the UK, the maximum rise is 220mm and the run is 220mm. In Australia, the max rise is 190mm and the min run is 240mm. Use our Stair Stringer Calculator for exact dimensions.

2. The 2R + T Rule

Master carpenters use the 2R + T rule to ensure comfortable stairs: (2 × Riser Height) + (1 × Tread Depth) = 24 to 25 inches (or 600-640mm).

This rule matches the average human stride. If your riser is 7 inches and your tread is 11 inches: (2 × 7) + 11 = 25 inches. This is the "sweet spot" for a natural, safe climb.

Understanding Stair Stringers

The stringer is the inclined structural member that supports the entire load of the stairs. It is notched to fit the treads and risers.

Stringer Length: Calculated using the Pythagorean theorem: √(Total Rise² + Total Run²).

Stringer Size: Standard residential stairs use a 2x12 board (1.5" x 11.25"). This allows for the notches while leaving a solid "throat" of at least 3.5 inches at the inside corner. Never use a 2x10, as the cuts will weaken the board and cause it to snap. Our Stair Stringer Calculator accounts for tread thickness and birdsmouth cuts.

Global Stair Codes: US vs. UK vs. Australia

Stair codes vary significantly around the world. If you are building internationally, you must comply with local regulations.

  • USA (IRC): Max riser 7.75", min tread 10", min headroom 6'8". Handrail height 34-38".
  • UK (BS 5395 / Part K): Max rise 220mm, min going 220mm, min headroom 2000mm. Handrail height 900-1000mm.
  • Australia (NCC): Max rise 190mm, min going 240mm, min headroom 2030mm. Handrail height 865-1015mm.

Our tools allow you to toggle between Imperial and Metric units, so you can calculate in the standard you are most comfortable with.

Baluster Spacing: The 4-Inch Sphere Rule

The gap between balusters is one of the most critical safety features of a staircase. In the US, the IRC mandates the 4-inch sphere rule: a 4-inch sphere must not be able to pass through any gap. This prevents small children from slipping through or getting their heads stuck.

In the UK, the maximum gap is 100mm. In Australia, it is 125mm.

To calculate the exact number of balusters and their spacing, you must account for the baluster width and the total railing length. Our Baluster Calculator does this instantly, ensuring you never fail inspection.

Stair Rails vs. Deck Rails

Balusters on a staircase must be installed perfectly vertical (plumb), even though the handrail is angled (raking). This means the bottom of each baluster must be cut at an angle to sit flush on the stair tread, and the top must be cut to match the slope of the handrail.

This is more complex than horizontal deck rails. Use our Baluster Calculator for exact spacing and lengths on stairs.

Handrails and Landings

Every staircase with more than 3 risers requires a continuous handrail on at least one side. The handrail must be easy to grip (not too bulky) and must be firmly anchored to the wall or to sturdy posts.

Handrails should be installed at a height of 34 to 38 inches (86-96 cm) above the stair nosings. They must also return to the wall at the top and bottom to prevent clothing from snagging.

A landing is a flat platform between flights of stairs. Landings are required when the total rise of a stair exceeds a certain height (12 feet in the US, 4 meters in the UK). They provide a rest area and a safe turn if the stairs change direction.

Stair Safety Checklist

Before finishing your stairs, verify the following:

  • All risers are equal in height (variance < 3/8 inch).
  • All treads are equal in depth.
  • Handrail is continuous and securely anchored.
  • Baluster gaps comply with the 4-inch sphere rule.
  • Headroom is at least 6'8" (80 inches) throughout.
  • Landing height and depth meet code.

Frequently Asked Questions

Stair Calculator FAQs

Direct answers to common questions about stair stringers, rise, run, and balusters.

01

What is the standard rise and run for stairs?
In the US, standard riser height is 7 to 7.75 inches with a tread run of 10 to 11 inches. In the UK, the max rise is 220mm and the going is 220mm. In Australia, the max rise is 190mm and the going is 240mm. Use our Stair Stringer Calculator for exact measurements.
02

How do I calculate stair stringer length?
Use the Pythagorean theorem: Stringer Length = √(Total Rise² + Total Run²). You must also account for the thickness of the tread and the ridge board. Our Stair Stringer Calculator does this math instantly.
03

How many balusters do I need for my railing?
Divide the railing length by the maximum gap allowed (4 inches in the US, 100mm in the UK, 125mm in AU), plus the baluster width. Our Baluster Calculator provides exact counts and spacing.
04

What is the 2R + T rule?
The 2R + T rule ensures comfortable stairs: (2 × Riser Height) + (1 × Tread Depth) should equal 24 to 25 inches (or 600-640mm). Our Stair Stringer Calculator checks this automatically.
05

What is the minimum headroom for stairs?
The US IRC requires a minimum of 6 feet 8 inches (80 inches) of headroom. The UK requires 2000mm. Australia requires 2030mm. Always check this before framing, as insufficient headroom fails inspection.
06

Can I use a 2x10 for a stair stringer?
Generally, no. A 2x10 is only 9.25 inches wide, leaving a thin throat when cut. A 2x12 (11.25 inches) is the standard material for stairs. Use our Stair Stringer Calculator for stringer length.
07

How do I cut stair stringers?
Use a framing square with stair gauges set at the riser and tread dimensions. Mark the sawtooth pattern along a 2x12 board and cut with a circular saw, finishing the inside corners with a hand saw. Our calculator provides the exact dimensions for the cuts.
