Introduction

Why a Construction Tool Needs a Disclaimer

BuildMetric is an independent publisher of construction calculators and technical reference material. Our tools are built from published formulas and standard engineering practice, and we take considerable care to make them accurate. But a calculator is a mathematical instrument, not a design authority — and the distance between a correct calculation and a compliant, safe, buildable roof, wall or foundation is bridged by site conditions, material tolerances, local amendments and the judgement of a competent professional. This disclaimer exists to define that boundary precisely.

When you use any BuildMetric calculator — whether it is a roof pitch converter, a concrete volume estimator, a stud count tool or a paint coverage calculator — you are supplying inputs that we cannot verify and receiving outputs that we cannot inspect against your project. Two users can enter identical dimensions and still need completely different answers, because one is working to the International Residential Code in Texas, another to BS 5534 in Manchester, and a third to AS 1684 in Queensland. Building codes are amended at national, state and municipal level, and they change on cycles we do not control. The numbers we publish reflect widely accepted formulas; they do not reflect the specific edition of the code that governs your site this month.

The content on this website — including written guides, worked examples, tables, diagrams and explanatory commentary — is provided for general information and educational purposes only. It does not constitute structural engineering advice, architectural design services, quantity surveying certification, legal advice, or a substitute for any of the above. Nothing on this site should be read as a representation that a particular material, assembly or detail is suitable for your project, your climate, your occupancy class or your insurance requirements. Where a decision carries structural, fire, waterproofing, accessibility or life-safety consequences, that decision must be made by a licensed or chartered professional who has reviewed the actual site and the actual drawings.

We also want to be candid about the practical reality of estimating. Material quantities returned by any calculator are theoretical figures based on nominal dimensions and ideal geometry. Real projects carry waste allowances, cutting losses, lap and overlay, breakage, delivery minimums, and dimensional variance in the supplied product. Our outputs are a starting point for a professional takeoff — never a purchase order. Similarly, our guides describe general practice; they cannot anticipate unusual loading, heritage constraints, party wall obligations, planning permission conditions or covenants attached to a specific title.

By continuing to use buildmetric.org, you acknowledge that you have read and understood this disclaimer, that you accept full responsibility for how you apply any information obtained here, and that you will verify all results against the relevant codes, standards and manufacturer instructions before construction begins. If you do not accept these terms, please discontinue use of the site and its tools. If anything in this document is unclear, write to us at contact@buildmetric.org — we would rather answer a question up front than correct a misunderstanding later.

Section 03

Calculator Accuracy & Limitations

BuildMetric calculators use published, widely accepted formulas — the same trigonometric and geometric relationships taught in engineering fundamentals. Where we reference code minimums, they are drawn from the current published edition of the relevant standard at the time of writing. We test each tool against known reference cases before publishing and re-review them periodically against new editions and errata.

Nevertheless, every calculator has inherent limitations. Our inputs assume ideal geometry, nominal dimensions and consistent material properties. In reality, sawn timber varies in actual size from its nominal size; concrete volumes assume perfect formwork; roof pitches assume straight rafters with no camber; ground conditions assume representative soil; membrane coverages assume flat, defect-free substrates. Where these assumptions do not hold — and on real sites they frequently do not — the calculator's output becomes a theoretical figure that must be adjusted on site by whoever is doing the work.

Some practical factors no online tool can fully account for:

  • Waste allowances — typically 5–15% depending on the material and the complexity of cuts.
  • Cutting losses on angled, hip-cut and valley members.
  • Delivery minimums and packaging sizes imposed by suppliers.
  • Damage in transit and site handling breakage.
  • Product tolerances permitted by the relevant manufacturing standard.
  • Installation overlap and lap required by the manufacturer's instructions.
  • Dimensional variance between manufacturers of the same nominal product.

We present figures rounded to practical construction precision — typically two decimal places for angles, whole numbers for quantities, and consistent metric-imperial conversion. Rounding errors at that scale are negligible for estimating purposes but can accumulate across many components. For critical dimensions — setting out, structural sizing, cutting lists — always recalculate or verify with a physical measurement taken on site.

Treat every BuildMetric output as a starting estimate to be verified, cross-checked and, where necessary, adjusted by a competent professional. Do not use a calculator result as the sole basis for ordering materials, setting out on site or finalising a design.

Remember

A calculator is a mathematical aid. Verification, adjustment and final approval are always your responsibility — or your professional's.

Section 04

Regional Code Differences

BuildMetric serves users in the United States, the United Kingdom and Australia, and we write for all three. These markets share a common engineering language but apply different codes, different units and different approval processes. A calculator result that is correct in one market may be non-compliant in another unless the underlying standard is checked.

United States. Residential work is typically governed by the International Residential Code (IRC) or the International Building Code (IBC), but adoption is state-by-state and often city-by-city. Local amendments can be significant — seismic provisions in California, high-wind requirements in Florida, snow-load provisions in the north-east, and energy-code overlays in nearly every jurisdiction. Metric and imperial units are both used, though residential construction remains predominantly imperial.

United Kingdom. Work is governed by the Building Regulations (England & Wales), the Building (Scotland) Regulations and the Building Regulations (Northern Ireland), supported by Approved Documents and British Standards such as BS 5534 for roofing and BS 5268/BS EN 1995 for timber. The UK is fully metric, and pitch is usually expressed as an angle in degrees. Wind and rain exposure is mapped regionally, which frequently drives stricter fixing and pitch requirements in the west and north.

Australia. The National Construction Code (NCC) operates alongside Australian Standards — notably AS 1684 for timber framing and AS 4055 for wind classification — with state-based variations and additional cyclonic provisions in the far north. Metric units are universal. Compliance is typically certified by a registered building surveyor, and Bushfire Attack Level (BAL) ratings can materially affect material and detailing requirements.

Because of these differences, every BuildMetric output must be re-checked against the specific code edition, local amendment and approval pathway that applies to your site. Where a UK user specifies 30°, a US user specifies 7:12 and an Australian user specifies a 30-degree pitch to AS 1684, they are all describing roughly the same geometry — but the compliance context is not the same. We cannot and do not warrant that content written for one market is valid in another.

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to the Questions We Hear Most

Seven direct answers about calculator accuracy, professional advice boundaries, regional code differences and how BuildMetric is funded and reviewed.

01 Are BuildMetric calculators accurate enough to rely on for a construction quote?

The mathematical output of a BuildMetric calculator is accurate because it is derived from published formulas and tested against reference cases. However, a construction quote also depends on site conditions, waste allowances, labour rates, material availability and local code requirements — none of which a calculator can fully account for. Use our outputs as a starting estimate, then adjust for waste and confirm with a professional takeoff before committing to a contract price.

02 Can I submit calculator results with a building permit application?

No. A building permit application requires drawings, calculations and specifications prepared or endorsed by a licensed professional — structural engineer, architect or registered building designer, depending on your jurisdiction. BuildMetric calculators are educational and estimating tools, not certified design deliverables. Submit only figures that have been reviewed and certified by the appropriate licensed professional.

03 Why do US, UK and Australian users get different answers from the same calculator?

Because of different units, codes and standards. The United States uses imperial units and follows the International Residential Code or International Building Code. The United Kingdom uses metric units and follows the Building Regulations alongside British Standards such as BS 5534. Australia uses metric units and follows the National Construction Code alongside Australian Standards such as AS 1684. Material minimum pitches, wind uplift, snow loads and waste factors differ between them. Our calculators apply universal mathematics, but where code thresholds matter, users must apply their own jurisdiction's values.

04 Do you provide structural engineering or architectural advice?

No. BuildMetric is a publisher of general information. We do not provide structural engineering, architectural design, quantity surveying certification, legal advice or planning guidance. Any decision involving structural, fire, waterproofing or life-safety consequences must be made by a licensed professional in your jurisdiction who has reviewed the actual site and the actual drawings.

05 Are BuildMetric tools really free, and how do you fund the site?

Yes, all BuildMetric calculators and written guides are free to use. The site is funded through display advertising and affiliate commissions on some product links. Advertising partners never influence calculator formulas, rankings or editorial conclusions. Any affiliate relationship is disclosed on the page where the link appears. If a placement is confusing, write to contact@buildmetric.org.

06 How do I report an error or suggest an improvement to a calculator?

Email contact@buildmetric.org with the page URL, what you observed and what you expected. Include screenshots and any source references, such as code clauses, standard sections or manufacturer data. We review reports within two business days and, where warranted, update the calculator, add an errata note or revise the surrounding guide. Corrections are logged in the page's version history.

07 Who writes and reviews BuildMetric content?

Content is produced by the BuildMetric Engineering Team, which includes quantity surveyors, structural engineers, licensed roofing contractors and technical editors with combined field experience across the United States, United Kingdom and Australia. Each calculator and guide is reviewed by at least one practising professional before publication, and is periodically re-reviewed against current code editions. Author names and credentials appear in the Trust Box on each page.
