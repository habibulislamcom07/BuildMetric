Whether you are building a new driveway, laying a patio base, or filling a flower bed, gravel is one of the most versatile and cost-effective landscaping materials available. However, unlike buying bags of cement or boxes of tiles, gravel is sold by weight (tons) or volume (cubic yards), which makes estimating much more complex.

The most common question we hear is: "How many tons of gravel do I need?" If you guess wrong, you could end up with a massive pile of leftover stone in your driveway, or worse, a half-finished project waiting for a delivery truck. This guide breaks down the exact mathematical formulas and density factors used by professional landscapers in the US, UK, and Australia to ensure you order exactly what you need.

1. The Math: Volume vs. Weight

Gravel is measured in volume (cubic feet or cubic yards) but sold by weight (tons or kilograms). To convert between the two, you must know the density of the specific type of gravel you are buying.

The formula for volume is simple:
Volume (cubic yards) = Length (ft) × Width (ft) × Depth (ft) ÷ 27
(We divide by 27 because there are 27 cubic feet in one cubic yard).

To find the weight, you multiply the volume by the gravel's density. Standard crushed stone has a density of about 1.4 to 1.5 tons per cubic yard. Pea gravel is lighter (around 1.35 tons/yd³), while river rock is heavier (up to 1.6 tons/yd³). Our calculator uses these exact density factors automatically.

Gravel Type Density (Tons/yd³) Best Used For
Pea Gravel 1.35 Playgrounds, walkways, decorative flower beds.
Crushed Stone (#57) 1.45 Driveway base, drainage, concrete mix.
River Rock 1.60 Dry creek beds, landscape borders.
Lava Rock 1.25 Lightweight ground cover, gas fire pits.

The Estimator's Rule: Always order 5% to 10% more gravel than your exact calculation. The ground beneath is rarely perfectly flat, and the gravel will compact slightly over time. Our calculator automatically adds a 10% waste factor as standard.

2. How Deep Should You Lay Gravel?

The depth of your gravel layer depends entirely on the application. Using the wrong depth is the most common reason a driveway fails or a garden looks messy.

  • Driveways (Residential): You need a minimum of 4 inches of compacted crushed stone. For a heavy-use driveway (RVs, trucks), increase this to 6-8 inches. It should be laid in two layers: a base layer of large stone, and a top layer of smaller stone.
  • Walkways and Paths: A depth of 2 to 3 inches is sufficient. Use a smaller gravel like pea gravel or decomposed granite for comfortable walking.
  • Patio Base: Underneath patio pavers, you need a solid base of 4 inches of compacted crushed stone, followed by 1 inch of sand for leveling.
  • Decorative Flower Beds: 2 inches of decorative gravel is enough to suppress weeds and retain moisture.

3. Driveway Base Preparation

You cannot just dump gravel onto bare dirt and expect it to last. A proper driveway requires a sub-base.

First, excavate the area to a depth equal to your gravel layer plus 2 inches (for the geotextile fabric). Compact the soil using a plate compactor. Lay down a heavy-duty geotextile fabric (weed barrier). This fabric is crucial; it prevents the gravel from sinking into the mud and stops weeds from growing up through the stone.

Next, lay a 3-inch layer of large, angular stone (like #3 or #4 crushed stone) as a base. Compact it thoroughly with a roller or plate compactor. Finally, add a 2-inch top layer of smaller, decorative crushed stone (like #57) for a smooth driving surface.

4. Regional Gravel Standards

Gravel sizing and terminology vary globally:

  • United States: Gravel is sized by numbers (e.g., #57, #67, #411). #57 is the standard for driveways. Sold by the ton or cubic yard.
  • United Kingdom: Gravel is often sold as "shingle," "ballast," or "MOT Type 1" (for base layers). Sold in bags (25kg or bulk bags of 850kg).
  • Australia: Crushed rock is sold as "DGB20" (Dense Graded Base) for driveways, or decorative "bush rock." Sold by the cubic meter or tonne.

5. How to Calculate the Number of Bags Needed

If you have a small project (like a walkway or a small flower bed), buying gravel by the truckload is not practical. You will need to buy it in bags from your local hardware store (like Home Depot, B&Q, or Bunnings).

Most bags of gravel contain 0.5 cubic feet (or 25kg in the UK/AU). To find out how many bags you need:

  1. Calculate your total volume in cubic feet (Length × Width × Depth in feet).
  2. Divide that volume by 0.5 (the size of one bag).

Example: A 10x10 foot patio with 2 inches of gravel.
Depth in feet = 2 / 12 = 0.167 ft.
Volume = 10 × 10 × 0.167 = 16.7 cubic feet.
16.7 / 0.5 = 34 bags.
Buying 34 bags will cost significantly more than ordering a bulk bag, but it saves you from having a massive pile of leftover gravel in your driveway.

6. Cost Estimation: Gravel Prices

The cost of gravel varies wildly depending on your location, the type of stone, and whether you buy in bulk or bags.

Material Costs (Bulk)

  • US: $30 to $60 per ton for standard crushed stone. Decorative stones (like river rock or lava rock) can cost $80 to $150+ per ton.
  • UK: £50 to £80 per bulk bag (approx 850kg) for standard gravel. Decorative shingle can be £100+ per bag.
  • Australia: AUD $50 to $90 per cubic meter for standard crushed rock.

Delivery Fees

This is the hidden cost that surprises most DIYers. If you cannot transport the gravel yourself, you must pay for delivery. A standard dump truck can carry about 10 to 15 tons of gravel. Delivery fees range from $50 to $150 depending on distance.

Pro Tip: If you only need 1 or 2 tons, it is often cheaper to rent a small trailer and pick it up yourself than to pay a minimum delivery fee.

7. Gravel vs. Other Landscaping Materials

Gravel is not the only option for your yard. Here is how it compares to other common materials:

  • Mulch: Organic and good for retaining moisture, but it decomposes and needs to be replaced every year or two. Gravel is permanent.
  • Pavers (Bricks/Concrete): More expensive and labor-intensive to install, but they offer a much harder surface for heavy foot traffic or vehicles. Gravel is a cheaper, easier base.
  • Asphalt/Concrete Driveways: Much more expensive upfront, but they offer a perfectly smooth surface. Gravel driveways require periodic raking and topping up.

8. Common Landscaping Mistakes to Avoid

Even seasoned landscapers make these mistakes. Avoiding them will save you from an ugly, expensive mess.

  1. Not using a weed barrier: If you skip the geotextile fabric, weeds will grow through the gravel within weeks. You will never be able to pull them out without making a mess.
  2. Using rounded gravel on a slope: Pea gravel and river rock are rounded; they roll. If you use them on a steep driveway or slope, they will wash away in the rain. Use angular crushed stone on slopes.
  3. Not compacting the base: If you don't compact the sub-base, the gravel will sink and create ruts under the weight of your car.
  4. Building a gravel bed next to a lawn without an edge: Gravel will inevitably spill into the grass and destroy your lawnmower blades. Install a metal or plastic edging to keep the gravel contained.

9. Preparing for Delivery

When ordering bulk gravel, the truck will dump it in a single pile. It is heavy (a 10-ton pile is immense). Make sure you clear an area on your driveway or yard where the truck can safely dump the load without crushing anything. Cover it with a tarp if rain is expected, as wet gravel is heavier and harder to move.

Gravel Questions Answered

Common questions about estimating gravel volume, weight, and driveway depth.

01 How many tons of gravel do I need for a driveway?
For a standard residential driveway, you need a depth of 4 inches of compacted crushed stone. For a 20x20 foot driveway (400 sq ft), 4 inches deep equals about 4.9 cubic yards. Since crushed stone weighs about 1.45 tons per cubic yard, you will need roughly 7.1 tons of gravel. Always add 10% for compaction and waste.
02 How much does a cubic yard of gravel weigh?
A cubic yard of gravel typically weighs between 1.35 and 1.6 tons, depending on the type of stone. Pea gravel is lighter (1.35 tons), while river rock is heavier (1.6 tons). Crushed stone (the most common type) weighs about 1.45 tons per cubic yard. Our calculator adjusts the weight automatically based on the type you select.
03 How deep should gravel be for a driveway?
The minimum depth for a residential driveway is 4 inches of compacted stone. For heavier vehicles (RVs, trucks) or soft soil, increase the depth to 6 to 8 inches. It is best practice to lay it in two layers: a 3-inch base of large angular stone, and a 2-inch top layer of smaller crushed stone.
04 Do I need a weed barrier under gravel?
Yes, absolutely. A geotextile weed barrier (woven landscape fabric) is essential. It stops weeds from growing up through the gravel, and it separates the gravel from the soil below, preventing the gravel from sinking into the mud over time. Without a barrier, your gravel will disappear and weeds will take over within a year.
05 What is the best gravel for a driveway?
For driveways, always use angular crushed stone (like #57 or #411 in the US, or MOT Type 1 in the UK). The angular edges lock together to create a stable surface. Avoid rounded stones like pea gravel or river rock on driveways; they will roll under tires and wash away in heavy rain.
06 How do I calculate how many bags of gravel I need?
Most bags of gravel contain 0.5 cubic feet. First, calculate the total volume in cubic feet (Length × Width × Depth in feet). Then, divide that number by 0.5. For example, a 10x10 area at 2 inches deep requires about 16.7 cubic feet, which means you need 34 bags. Buying bulk is much cheaper for anything over 20 bags.
07 How often do I need to add gravel to my driveway?
With proper base preparation and a weed barrier, a gravel driveway should last 10 to 15 years. However, you will likely need to "top up" the gravel every 2 to 3 years with a fresh layer of stone to replace material that has been displaced by tires or swept away by rain. Raking the gravel back into place after winter is also a good practice.
