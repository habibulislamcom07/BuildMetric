Quick Answer: How Many Bags of Concrete Do I Need?

To calculate the number of concrete bags you need, first find the total volume of your project (Length × Width × Depth). Convert that volume to cubic feet, then divide it by the yield of your bag size. An 80lb bag yields 0.60 cu ft, a 60lb bag yields 0.45 cu ft, and a 50lb bag yields 0.375 cu ft. Always add a 5% waste factor.

How to Calculate Concrete Bags for a Slab

For a standard slab, multiply Length × Width × Thickness (in feet) to get cubic feet. Divide that total by the bag yield. For example, a 10x10 slab at 4 inches thick is 33.3 cubic feet. Using 80lb bags (0.60 cu ft yield), you will need 56 bags. Using 60lb bags (0.45 cu ft), you will need 74 bags.

Calculating the exact number of concrete bags is critical for projects that are too small for a ready-mix truck but too large to "eyeball." Whether you are pouring a 10x10 shed slab, setting fence posts, or building a small patio, ordering too few bags will result in a cold joint (a weak seam where old and new concrete meet). Ordering too many bags wastes money and leaves you with heavy, unused materials.

Concrete Bag Yields: 80lb vs. 60lb vs. 50lb

The yield of a concrete bag depends on its weight. An 80lb bag yields approximately 0.60 cubic feet, a 60lb bag yields 0.45 cubic feet, and a 40lb bag yields 0.30 cubic feet. Using heavier bags reduces the total number of bags you need, saving you time and physical effort during mixing.

Most bagged concrete sold in the US is a pre-blended mix of Portland cement, sand, and gravel. You only need to add water. However, the "yield" (the volume of concrete you get per bag) is standardized by the manufacturer. Here is the standard table:

When to Use Bags vs. Ready-Mix Concrete

As a general rule, if your project requires more than 1 cubic yard of concrete (roughly 45 bags of 80lb mix), you should order ready-mix concrete from a supplier. Mixing dozens of bags by hand in a wheelbarrow is exhausting and can lead to inconsistent batches. For smaller jobs, bags are cost-effective and convenient.

Ready-mix concrete is delivered by a truck and poured directly into your forms. It is mixed to exact specifications (PSI) at the plant, ensuring uniform strength and curing. However, ready-mix suppliers often have a minimum order quantity (usually 1 cubic yard), which makes it impractical for small jobs.

If you are using bags, ensure you have a sturdy wheelbarrow or mixing tub, a hoe, and access to water. Our Concrete Calculator can also help you determine the total volume in cubic yards.

The Correct Water-to-Concrete Ratio

The most common mistake when using bagged concrete is adding too much water. For most pre-blended mixes, you should add only 2.5 to 3 quarts of water per 80lb bag. Adding more water makes the concrete easier to pour but drastically reduces its final compressive strength and causes surface cracking.

Water is essential for the chemical reaction (hydration) that hardens the cement. However, only a specific amount of water is needed. The ideal water-to-cement ratio is between 0.4 and 0.5.

Adding extra water creates "bleeding" (water rising to the surface) and weakens the mix. The concrete will look dry and crumbly initially, but as you mix, it will come together. If it is too stiff, add water in very small increments (a cup at a time), not a full hose.

How to Mix Bagged Concrete Properly

To mix concrete by hand, pour the dry mix into a wheelbarrow or mixing tub, form a "crater" in the center, and add half the required water. Mix with a hoe, gradually pulling the dry mix into the water. Add the remaining water slowly until the mix reaches a thick, oatmeal-like consistency.

For larger projects (10+ bags), consider renting a portable concrete mixer. A mixer ensures a more uniform blend and saves significant physical effort.

Never mix concrete on a bare dirt surface. The dirt will contaminate the mix, and the water will drain away. Use a wheelbarrow, a plastic mixing tub, or a clean plywood board.

Once mixed, the concrete must be placed within 30-60 minutes (before it begins to set). If it starts to harden, do not add more water to "revive" it. Discard it and mix a fresh batch.

Curing: Why the First 7 Days Matter

Concrete does not dry; it cures. The chemical reaction continues for weeks, but the first 7 days are critical. During this time, you must keep the concrete moist to prevent it from drying too fast. Mist it with water daily or cover it with plastic sheeting. Do not walk on it for at least 24-48 hours.

If the concrete dries out too quickly (especially in hot, windy weather), the chemical reaction stops prematurely, resulting in weak, dusty concrete.

For small slabs, watering the surface 2-3 times a day is sufficient. For larger slabs, cover with a curing compound or plastic sheets.

Wait at least 7 days before placing heavy loads on the concrete. It will reach 99% of its design strength after 28 days. For a complete guide on curing and temperature limits, check out our Concrete Calculator.

Frequently Asked Questions

Concrete Bag FAQs

Direct answers to common questions about bag yields, mixing, and quantities.

01

How many 80lb bags of concrete for a 10x10 slab?
For a 10x10 slab at 4 inches thick (33.3 cubic feet), you will need approximately 56 bags of 80lb concrete. However, for projects this size, ordering ready-mix concrete by the cubic yard is usually more cost-effective. Use the calculator above for exact volumes.
02

How many bags of concrete in a cubic yard?
One cubic yard equals 27 cubic feet. An 80lb bag yields 0.60 cu ft, so there are exactly 45 bags of 80lb concrete in a cubic yard. For 60lb bags, it takes 60 bags. For 50lb bags, it takes 72 bags.
03

How much water do I add to an 80lb bag of concrete?
You should add about 2.5 to 3 quarts of water per 80lb bag. Add half the water first, mix, then add the rest slowly. Never exceed 3 quarts, as too much water weakens the concrete and causes cracking.
04

Is it cheaper to mix my own concrete or order ready-mix?
For projects under 1 cubic yard (45 bags), mixing your own is cheaper and easier. For projects over 1 cubic yard, ready-mix concrete is generally cheaper per yard and saves hours of manual labor. Renting a portable mixer can help for mid-size projects.
05

Can I use bagged concrete for a driveway?
Technically yes, but it is not recommended for full driveways. A driveway requires high-strength concrete (4000+ PSI) and precise water ratios to handle vehicle loads. Ready-mix is delivered to this specification. Bagged concrete is better suited for footings, fence posts, and small patio slabs.
06

How long does bagged concrete take to cure?
Bagged concrete sets in 24 to 48 hours but reaches full strength after 28 days. You can walk on it after 24 hours, but wait at least 7 days before placing heavy loads on it. Keep it moist for the first 7 days for best results.
07

What happens if I add too much water to bagged concrete?
Adding too much water dilutes the cement paste, reducing the compressive strength of the concrete by up to 50%. It also causes shrinkage cracks and a dusty surface. Always follow the bag's water instructions precisely.
