Installing a new lawn is one of the most transformative landscaping projects you can do. It instantly boosts your home's curb appeal and provides a safe space for kids and pets. But unlike seeding, sod requires a precise estimate. Sod is a perishable product; if you leave it stacked in your driveway for too long, it will overheat, turn yellow, and die.

The most common question we hear is: "How much sod do I need?" If you under-order, you'll have bare patches and have to make another trip. If you over-order, you're wasting money on heavy rolls that will likely die before they can be used. This guide walks through the exact math and soil preparation steps used by professional landscapers in the US, UK, and Australia.

1. The Math: Calculating Sod Area

Sod is sold in rolls (usually 5 to 10 square feet each) or by the pallet (usually covering 400 to 500 square feet). To calculate the exact quantity:

  1. Measure the area: For a rectangular lawn, multiply Length × Width. For irregular shapes, divide the lawn into smaller rectangles, circles, or triangles, calculate each, and add them together.
  2. Add a waste factor: Always add 5% to 10% extra for cuts around trees, curves, and garden beds. If your lawn is highly irregular (curves, many obstacles), increase this to 15%.
  3. Convert to rolls/pallets: Divide the final area by the coverage of your chosen sod roll (e.g., 10 sq ft) or pallet (e.g., 400 sq ft).

Estimator's Note: Sod rolls shrink slightly after they are cut at the farm. A roll labeled "10 sq ft" may actually measure 9.5 sq ft upon delivery. Our calculator assumes standard sizing, but always round up to the nearest full roll.

2. Choosing the Right Sod Type

The success of your lawn depends heavily on choosing a grass that thrives in your climate.

Cool-Season Grasses (US North, UK, AU South)

These grasses grow best in spring and autumn and can survive freezing winters.

  • Tall Fescue: Deep-rooted and durable. Good for high-traffic areas. Stays green year-round in mild climates.
  • Kentucky Bluegrass: Beautiful, fine texture but requires lots of water and maintenance.
  • Perennial Ryegrass: Fast-germinating and durable but less heat-tolerant.

Warm-Season Grasses (US South, AU North)

These grasses thrive in hot summers and go dormant (turn brown) in winter.

  • Bermuda: Extremely tough and drought-tolerant. Self-repairs. Used on sports fields.
  • St. Augustine: Broad blades, shade-tolerant, but requires lots of water.
  • Zoysia: Slow-growing but very dense. Excellent for low-maintenance lawns.
  • Buffalo Grass (AU): Native, drought-tolerant, and extremely low maintenance.

3. Soil Preparation: The Secret to a Healthy Lawn

You cannot lay sod on hard, compacted dirt and expect it to thrive. The roots of the sod must penetrate the soil beneath it within days.

Step 1: Remove the Old Lawn. Use a sod cutter or a spade to remove the existing grass. Do not simply rototill it into the soil—the old grass will decompose and create an uneven surface.

Step 2: Till and Amend. Till the top 4-6 inches of soil. Mix in organic matter (compost) to improve drainage and nutrient content. This is also the time to add a starter fertilizer.

Step 3: Grade and Compact. Rake the soil smooth, ensuring it slopes away from your house for drainage. Lightly compact the soil with a roller. This prevents the sod from sinking and becoming uneven.

Step 4: Water Before Laying. Lightly mist the soil before laying the sod. The sod needs moisture underneath to send roots down quickly.

4. Regional Sod Standards

  • US: Sod is sold in rolls (10 sq ft) or pallets (400-500 sq ft). Delivery is usually within 24 hours of cutting.
  • UK: Sold in 1m² rolls, often stacked on a pallet of 50m². Turf must be laid within 24-48 hours.
  • Australia: Sold in rolls (approx 1 sq meter), often palletized. Buffalo grass is the most popular sod type.

5. Step-by-Step Installation Guide

Sod installation is physically demanding, but it is straightforward. The goal is to lay the sod within 24 hours of delivery to prevent it from drying out or overheating.

  1. Start at a Straight Edge: Begin laying sod along a straight line (like your driveway or a sidewalk). This serves as your anchor.
  2. Stagger the Joints: Just like laying bricks, stagger the seams of the rolls. Do not align the ends of adjacent rows. This prevents the seams from opening up and creating gaps.
  3. Push the Rolls Tightly Together: Sod shrinks as it dries. Push the rolls tightly together so there are no visible gaps. You can use a utility knife to trim around curves, trees, and flower beds.
  4. Roll the Sod: Use a heavy water-filled roller to press the sod firmly into the soil. This ensures good root-to-soil contact, which is critical for survival.
  5. Water Immediately: Water the new lawn thoroughly immediately after rolling. The soil beneath the sod should be soaked to a depth of at least 1 inch.

6. The Crucial Watering Schedule

The number one reason new sod dies is lack of water. For the first two weeks, your sod requires constant moisture.

  • Week 1: Water 2 to 3 times per day for short periods (about 10-15 minutes per zone). The goal is to keep the sod and the top inch of soil constantly wet.
  • Week 2: Reduce watering to once a day, but water deeper (about 20-30 minutes). This encourages the roots to grow downward into the soil.
  • Week 3: Water deeply every 2 to 3 days. At this point, you can gently tug on a corner of the sod; if it resists, the roots have taken hold.
  • Week 4+: Transition to a normal watering schedule (usually 1 inch of water per week, either from rain or irrigation).

Pro Tip: Water in the early morning to reduce evaporation. Avoid watering in the evening, as leaving the grass wet overnight can promote fungal diseases.

7. Cost Estimation: Sod Prices

Sod is one of the more expensive landscaping materials upfront, but it provides an instant lawn.

Material Costs

  • US: Standard sod ranges from $0.30 to $0.80 per square foot. A full pallet (400-500 sq ft) costs $150 to $400.
  • UK: Turf costs about £5 to £10 per square meter. A bulk bag (50m²) costs £250 to £500.
  • Australia: Expect to pay AUD $8 to $15 per square meter, depending on the grass type (Buffalo is more expensive than Kikuyu).

Labor Costs

If you hire a professional to install the sod (including soil prep), expect to pay:

  • US: $1.00 to $2.00 per square foot (installed).
  • UK: £15 to £25 per square meter (installed).
  • Australia: AUD $20 to $40 per square meter (installed).

8. Common Sod Installation Mistakes

Avoid these critical errors to ensure your new lawn survives:

  1. Delaying Installation: Sod is a living plant. If left stacked for more than 48 hours, it will begin to compost and die. Install it immediately.
  2. Not Prepping the Soil: Laying sod over rocks, weeds, or hard dirt will result in dead patches and an uneven surface.
  3. Skimping on Water: New sod needs a massive amount of water for the first two weeks. Do not rely on rainfall alone.
  4. Walking on It: Stay off the new sod for at least two weeks to allow the roots to establish themselves.

By using our calculator to precisely estimate your sod quantities and following the correct watering and preparation steps, you can install a beautiful, healthy lawn that will last for years.

Sod Questions Answered

Common questions about estimating sod rolls, pallets, and lawn preparation.

01 How much does a pallet of sod cover?
A standard pallet of sod in the US typically covers 400 to 500 square feet. In the UK and Australia, sod (turf) is often sold by the square meter, with a standard pallet covering around 50 square meters. Always confirm the exact coverage with your local supplier before ordering.
02 How many square feet is a roll of sod?
A standard roll of sod in the US covers 10 square feet (usually 2 feet wide by 5 feet long). However, larger rolls (often called "big rolls") can cover 18 to 20 square feet. In the UK and Australia, turf rolls are typically cut to 1 square meter.
03 How long can sod sit before it dies?
Sod is a living product and should be installed within 24 to 48 hours of delivery. If it sits in a stack for too long, the center of the pallet will heat up (a process called "composting"), causing the sod to turn yellow and die. Keep it in a shaded area and lightly water the stack if you cannot install it immediately.
04 Do I need to rototill before laying sod?
Yes. You must loosen the top 4-6 inches of soil to allow the sod roots to penetrate. If you lay sod over hard, compacted dirt, the roots will not be able to grow down, and the sod will die. Tilling also allows you to mix in compost and level the ground.
05 How often should I water new sod?
For the first two weeks, you must keep the sod and the soil beneath it constantly moist. Water 2 to 3 times per day for short periods. After the roots take hold (usually around week 3), reduce watering to every 2 to 3 days, but water deeper to encourage deep root growth.
06 What is the best time of year to lay sod?
Sod can be laid anytime the ground is not frozen. The best time is during the active growing season for your grass type. For cool-season grasses (Fescue, Bluegrass), early autumn or early spring is best. For warm-season grasses (Bermuda, St. Augustine), late spring to early summer is ideal.
07 How do I calculate sod for an irregularly shaped lawn?
Break the lawn into simple geometric shapes (rectangles, circles, triangles). Calculate the area of each shape, then add them together. For circles, use the formula πr². Once you have the total area, add a 10% to 15% waste factor because irregular shapes require more cuts and produce more unusable offcuts.
