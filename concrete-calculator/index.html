What Is a Concrete Calculator?

A concrete calculator is a tool that estimates how much concrete you need for a project — measured in cubic yards, cubic metres, or bags of pre-mixed concrete. Instead of guessing and ending up with too little (which forces an extra trip to the store and creates a visible cold joint) or too much (which wastes money), you enter your project's dimensions and the calculator returns the exact volume required.

Concrete is sold in three main ways: ready-mix delivered by truck (measured in cubic yards or cubic metres), pre-mixed bags from a hardware store (measured in pounds or kilograms), and bulk dry ingredients you mix yourself. This calculator handles all three — it returns volume in every major unit and tells you how many bags of each standard size you would need.

How to Use This Concrete Calculator

  1. Choose the shape. Most DIY projects use a rectangular slab (patio, driveway, footing). For a pier, post or pillar, choose the round column option.
  2. Select your unit system. Use the toggle at the top to switch between Imperial (feet, inches) and Metric (metres, centimetres). Your preference is saved for next time.
  3. Enter the dimensions. For a slab, provide length, width and thickness. For a column, provide diameter and height. Use the same units shown on each field.
  4. Set a waste allowance. We default to 10%, which is the standard recommendation. Use 5% for perfectly smooth subgrade and 15% for rough or uneven ground.
  5. Click Calculate. The result shows volume in cubic feet, cubic yards, cubic metres and litres — plus the number of bags needed for common pre-mix sizes.

Formula Used by This Calculator

The volume calculation depends on the shape you choose. All inputs are converted to feet internally before the final volume is computed, then converted back to your preferred unit system.

Rectangular Slab (Patio, Driveway, Footing)

Volume = Length × Width × Thickness All measurements must be in the same unit.
Divide cubic feet by 27 to get cubic yards.
Multiply cubic feet by 0.0283168 to get cubic metres.

Round Column (Pier, Post, Pillar)

Volume = π × r² × Height Where r = radius (diameter ÷ 2).
π (pi) ≈ 3.14159.
Result is in the same cubic unit as your input.

Waste Allowance

Final Volume = Volume × (1 + Waste% ÷ 100) Standard recommendation: 10%.

Worked Example

Suppose you are pouring a patio slab that is 10 feet long, 10 feet wide and 4 inches thick.

  1. Convert thickness to feet: 4 ÷ 12 = 0.333 ft
  2. Calculate volume in cubic feet: 10 × 10 × 0.333 = 33.33 cu ft
  3. Convert to cubic yards: 33.33 ÷ 27 = 1.235 cu yd
  4. Apply 10% waste: 1.235 × 1.10 = 1.358 cu yd
  5. Bags needed (80 lb): 33.33 × 1.10 ÷ 0.6 ≈ 62 bags

So for this patio, you would order approximately 1.36 cubic yards of ready-mix concrete — or buy 62 bags of 80 lb pre-mix.

Metric equivalent: 3.05 m × 3.05 m × 10 cm = 0.93 m³, plus 10% waste = 1.02 m³. In 50 kg bags, that is approximately 29 bags.

Standard Concrete Slab Thicknesses

Choosing the correct thickness is critical for structural integrity. Below are widely accepted standards used by contractors across North America, Europe and Australia.

  • 4 inches (10 cm) — patios, walkways, garden paths, shed floors
  • 5 inches (12.5 cm) — residential driveways with occasional heavy vehicles
  • 6 inches (15 cm) — driveways, garage floors, RV pads
  • 8–12 inches (20–30 cm) — footings, foundation walls, structural columns
Pro Tip: Always pour concrete on a compacted, well-drained subgrade. Weak subgrade means thicker concrete is needed — sometimes 2 inches extra — to prevent cracking.
