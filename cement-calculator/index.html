Concrete Calculator

What Is a Concrete Calculator?

A concrete calculator is a tool that estimates how much concrete you need for a project — measured in cubic yards, cubic metres, or bags of pre-mixed concrete. Instead of guessing and ending up with too little concrete (which forces an extra trip to the store and creates a visible cold joint) or too much (which wastes money), you enter your project's dimensions and the calculator returns the exact volume required.

Concrete quantity depends on several factors: the shape of the pour (slab, footing, column, stair), the dimensions, the thickness or depth, the mix design, and the waste allowance for spillage and uneven subgrade. This calculator takes all of these into account so you get a reliable estimate before you order.

Concrete is one of the most widely used construction materials in the world. It is used in foundations, footings, slabs, driveways, patios, sidewalks, columns, beams, stairs, retaining walls, and countless other structures. Whether you are pouring a small garden path or a large commercial foundation, accurate concrete estimation saves time, money, and frustration.

Whether you are pouring a driveway in Texas, laying a patio in London, building a deck footing in Sydney, or renovating a basement in Toronto, this calculator gives you an accurate estimate in both imperial and metric units.

How to Use This Concrete Calculator

Using this concrete calculator is straightforward. Follow these six steps:

  1. Choose the shape. Most DIY projects use a rectangular slab (patio, driveway, footing). For a pier, post or pillar, choose the round column option. For a staircase, choose the stair option.
  2. Select your unit system. Use the toggle at the top to switch between Imperial (feet, inches) and Metric (metres, centimetres). Your preference is saved for next time.
  3. Enter the dimensions. For a slab, provide length, width and thickness. For a column, provide diameter and height. For a stair, provide width, rise, run and number of steps. Use the same units shown on each field.
  4. Set a waste allowance. We default to 10%, which is the standard recommendation. Use 5% for perfectly smooth subgrade and 15% for rough or uneven ground.
  5. Choose your concrete type. The calculator supports ready-mix (by volume) and pre-mixed bags in 80 lb, 60 lb, 50 kg and 25 kg sizes.
  6. Click Calculate. The result shows volume in cubic feet, cubic yards, cubic metres and litres — plus the number of bags needed for common pre-mix sizes.
Pro Tip: Always order 1–2 extra bags beyond the calculated quantity. Concrete can be damaged during transport, spilled during pouring, or wasted due to uneven subgrade. Having spares on hand means you can finish the pour without a cold joint.

Formula Used by This Calculator

All calculations are performed internally in metric units, then converted back to your preferred unit system for display. The following formulas are used:

Step 1 — Rectangular Slab Volume

Volume = Length × Width × Thickness

All measurements must be in the same unit. Divide cubic feet by 27 to get cubic yards. Multiply cubic feet by 0.0283168 to get cubic metres.

Step 2 — Round Column Volume

Volume = π × r² × Height

Where r = radius (diameter ÷ 2). π (pi) = 3.14159. Result is in the same cubic unit as your input.

Step 3 — Staircase Volume

Volume = Steps × Width × Rise × Run ÷ 2

This formula assumes a standard staircase profile. Each step is treated as a triangular prism.

Step 4 — Waste Allowance

Final Volume = Volume × (1 + Waste% ÷ 100)

Standard recommendation: 10%.

Step 5 — Bags of Concrete

Bags = Final Volume ÷ Yield per Bag

80 lb bag = 0.6 cu ft (0.017 m³) | 60 lb bag = 0.45 cu ft (0.0127 m³) | 50 kg bag = 1.25 cu ft (0.0353 m³) | 25 kg bag = 0.625 cu ft (0.0177 m³)

Understanding the Variables

  • Length, Width, Thickness: Dimensions of the pour, all in the same unit.
  • Radius: Half the diameter of a round column.
  • Rise: Vertical height of each stair step.
  • Run: Horizontal depth of each stair tread.
  • Waste %: Extra material for spillage, uneven subgrade and future repairs.
  • Yield per Bag: Volume of concrete produced by one bag of pre-mix.

Why Round Up, Not Down?

Concrete calculations involve rounding in several places: bags, cubic yards and cubic metres. Always round up, never down. If your calculation gives 1.23 cubic yards, order 1.25 cubic yards (ready-mix is sold in 0.25-yard increments). If it gives 55.2 bags, order 56. Running short mid-pour causes cold joints that weaken the slab permanently.

Concrete Mix Types

  • Standard concrete (3000 PSI): General-purpose, for patios, walkways and footings.
  • High-strength concrete (4000 PSI): Driveways, garage floors and structural elements.
  • Fast-setting concrete: Fence posts and urgent repairs (sets in 20–40 minutes).
  • Crack-resistant concrete: Adds fibers to reduce cracking in slabs.
  • High-early-strength concrete: Reaches full strength in 3–7 days instead of 28.

Worked Example

Let us work through three examples — a simple patio slab, a round footing pier, and a small staircase.

Example 1 — Patio Slab (10 ft × 12 ft, 4 in thick, 10% waste)
  1. Convert thickness to feet: 4 ÷ 12 = 0.333 ft
  2. Calculate volume in cubic feet: 10 × 12 × 0.333 = 40 cu ft
  3. Convert to cubic yards: 40 ÷ 27 = 1.481 cu yd
  4. Apply 10% waste: 1.481 × 1.10 = 1.629 cu yd
  5. Bags needed (80 lb): 40 × 1.10 ÷ 0.6 = 74 bags

Result: You need approximately 1.63 cubic yards of concrete — or about 74 bags of 80 lb pre-mix.

Example 2 — Round Column Footing (12 in diameter, 8 ft high, 10% waste)
  1. Convert diameter to feet: 12 ÷ 12 = 1 ft
  2. Radius: 1 ÷ 2 = 0.5 ft
  3. Calculate volume in cubic feet: π × 0.5² × 8 = 6.28 cu ft
  4. Convert to cubic yards: 6.28 ÷ 27 = 0.233 cu yd
  5. Apply 10% waste: 0.233 × 1.10 = 0.256 cu yd
  6. Bags needed (60 lb): 6.28 × 1.10 ÷ 0.45 = 16 bags

Result: You need approximately 0.26 cubic yards of concrete — or about 16 bags of 60 lb pre-mix.
