International Cement & Concrete Estimation Guide

Accurate material estimation requires accounting for international unit conversions, local packaging standards, and dry volume shrinkage factors. Whether following American Concrete Institute (ACI 318), Indian Standards (IS 456), or Eurocode (BS EN 1992) guidelines, dry ingredients shrink significantly upon adding water due to void air displacement.

Volumetric Conversion & Shrinkage Factors

  • Concrete Dry Factor (1.54): Dry concrete ingredients reduce by approximately 35% in volume when water is mixed. Multiply wet concrete volume by 1.54 to determine required dry batch material.
  • Mortar Dry Factor (1.33): Brickwork and plastering mortar reduces by approximately 25% when mixed. Multiply wet volume by 1.33 for dry material estimations.
  • Global Bag Standards: 50 kg (Asia/Europe), 94 lb (US Portland), 40 kg (UK/AU retail), and 25 kg (DIY pre-mix).

International Mix Grades & Ratios

  • M15 / C15 (1 : 2 : 4): General non-structural slabs, garden paths, and patio bases.
  • M20 / C20 (1 : 1.5 : 3): Standard structural concrete for foundations, footings, and slabs.
  • M25 / C25 (1 : 1 : 2): Heavy-duty structural concrete for reinforced columns, beams, and retaining walls.

Worked Calculation Example

Example — Concrete Slab (10 m × 10 m, 100 mm thick, M20 Grade, 10% waste)
  1. Convert thickness to metres: 100 mm = 0.1 m
  2. Calculate wet volume: 10 × 10 × 0.1 = 10 m³
  3. Add 10% waste margin: 10 × 1.10 = 11 m³ total wet volume
  4. Apply dry shrinkage factor (1.54): 11 × 1.54 = 16.94 m³ dry volume required
  5. Mix Breakdown (M20 = 1:1.5:3, total 5.5 parts): Cement Volume = 16.94 × (1 / 5.5) = 3.08 m³
  6. Calculate mass and bags: 3.08 m³ × 1,440 kg/m³ = 4,435.2 kg → 4,435.2 ÷ 50 kg = 89 bags (50 kg each)
