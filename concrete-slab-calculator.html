How to Calculate Concrete for a Slab (Step-by-Step)

Whether you are pouring a small garden shed base in Australia, a driveway in the UK, or a large patio in the US, getting your concrete volume right is critical. Ordering too little concrete means you will have cold joints (weak spots) where the pour stopped. Ordering too much wastes your hard-earned money.

The formula for calculating concrete volume is simple: Volume = Length × Width × Thickness. However, the complexity arises when dealing with different units of measurement (Feet, Inches, Meters, Centimeters) and converting that volume into practical purchasing units like Cubic Yards or Pre-mixed Bags.

Step 1: Measure Your Dimensions

Start by measuring the length and width of the area where the slab will be poured. Ensure your measurements are accurate to the nearest inch or centimeter. Next, determine the thickness (depth) of the slab. A standard residential patio is usually 4 inches thick, while a driveway requires 5 to 6 inches for heavy vehicles.

Step 2: Apply the Volume Formula (US Standard)

If you are measuring in feet and inches (US standard), you must first convert the thickness from inches to feet by dividing it by 12.

Volume in Cubic Feet = Length (ft) × Width (ft) × (Thickness (in) ÷ 12)

Once you have the volume in cubic feet, you convert it to Cubic Yards (the standard unit for ordering ready-mix concrete in the US). Since one cubic yard contains 27 cubic feet:

Volume in Cubic Yards = Total Cubic Feet ÷ 27

Step 3: Apply the Volume Formula (UK/Metric)

In the UK and Australia, concrete is typically ordered in Cubic Meters. If you measure in meters and centimeters, convert the thickness from centimeters to meters by dividing by 100.

Volume in Cubic Meters = Length (m) × Width (m) × (Thickness (cm) ÷ 100)

Step 4: Calculate Pre-mixed Bags

If you are doing a small DIY project, you might use pre-mixed bags instead of ready-mix trucks. In the US, 80lb bags yield about 0.60 cubic feet, and 60lb bags yield about 0.45 cubic feet.

  • Number of 80lb Bags = Total Cubic Feet ÷ 0.60
  • Number of 60lb Bags = Total Cubic Feet ÷ 0.45

Step 5: Add a Waste Factor

Never order the exact amount you calculated. Ground is rarely perfectly level, and some concrete will be lost during pouring and finishing. Always add a 5% to 10% waste factor to your final volume. Our calculator above automatically adds a 10% waste factor to ensure you are covered.

Why Accurate Concrete Calculation Matters (The Cost of Mistakes)

In construction, concrete is one of the most unforgiving materials. Once it starts to set, there is no going back. If you order too little concrete for a continuous pour, you risk creating "cold joints" — weak points between the old and new concrete that can lead to structural failure, cracks, and water leakage over time.

On the other hand, over-ordering concrete is a direct waste of money. Ready-mix concrete trucks charge for the full load they bring, and dumping excess concrete is not only expensive (due to environmental regulations in the US, UK, and Australia) but also a logistical headache. Our calculator ensures you get the goldilocks amount—just enough, plus a safe waste margin.

Types of Concrete: Ready-Mix vs. Pre-Mixed Bags

Depending on the size of your project, you will need to choose a different supply method. Here is a breakdown of what professionals use:

1. Ready-Mix Concrete (Large Projects)

For slabs larger than 1 cubic yard (or 1 cubic meter), ready-mix is the most economical and practical choice. A large truck delivers the concrete directly to your site. You must have the site prepared before the truck arrives, as most companies only allow 10-15 minutes of pouring time before charging extra.

2. Pre-Mixed Bags (Small DIY Projects)

If you are pouring small footings, fence posts, or a tiny patio, pre-mixed bags (like Quikrete in the US or Blue Circle in the UK) are perfect. You just add water and mix in a wheelbarrow. Note: Mixing more than 1 cubic yard by hand is incredibly labor-intensive and usually not recommended.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about concrete slabs, measurements, and ordering.

How many 80lb bags of concrete do I need for a 10x10 slab?
For a 10x10 slab at 4 inches thick, you will need approximately 1.23 cubic yards of concrete. This equals about 56 bags of 80lb concrete (or 75 bags of 60lb). Our calculator above automatically adds a 10% waste factor, which means you would safely need around 62 bags.
How thick should a concrete slab be for a driveway?
A standard residential driveway should be at least 4 to 5 inches thick. However, if you have heavy vehicles (like a large truck or RV), a 6-inch thickness with reinforced rebar is highly recommended. For standard patios or shed bases, 4 inches is usually sufficient.
What is the difference between 60lb and 80lb concrete bags?
An 80lb bag yields about 0.60 cubic feet of concrete, while a 60lb bag yields about 0.45 cubic feet. 80lb bags are heavier but require fewer bags to reach your total volume, saving you time on mixing. 60lb bags are easier to carry and mix for smaller jobs.
How much does a cubic yard of concrete cover?
One cubic yard of concrete will cover an area of 81 square feet at a thickness of 4 inches. If your slab is 5 inches thick, it will cover 65 square feet, and at 6 inches thick, it will cover 54 square feet.
Do I need rebar or wire mesh for my slab?
For most residential slabs (patios, shed bases), wire mesh is recommended to prevent cracking. For driveways or structural slabs, rebar (reinforcing bar) is highly recommended. Placing the reinforcement in the middle of the slab is crucial for maximum strength.
How long does it take for a concrete slab to cure?
Concrete usually sets within 24 to 48 hours, meaning you can walk on it. However, it takes about 28 days to reach full structural strength. You should avoid driving heavy vehicles on a new driveway for at least 7 days.
Can I pour concrete in cold weather or rain?
It is not recommended to pour concrete in heavy rain, as it will wash out the cement paste and weaken the slab. For cold weather, concrete can be poured if the temperature is above 40°F (5°C) and rising. If freezing temperatures are expected, special additives or blankets must be used to prevent the concrete from freezing before it cures.
