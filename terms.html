Section 04

Calculator Use & Accuracy

The calculators on this site are provided as estimating aids. They apply well-established mathematical formulas and geometric relationships, and we make reasonable efforts to test each tool against reference cases before publishing. But an accurate calculation is not the same as a compliant or buildable outcome, and this section explains the boundary between the two.

4.1 What the calculators do

Each calculator on BuildMetric applies a defined mathematical formula to the inputs you supply, and returns a numerical result. The formula is documented in the accompanying guide, and can be derived from first principles. The mathematics is universal — a ratio is a ratio whether you are in Texas or Tasmania. What varies is the interpretation of that mathematics against a specific code, standard or manufacturer's installation manual.

4.2 What the calculators do not do

The calculators do not:

  • Certify that a particular geometry, material or assembly is compliant with any specific code, standard or regulation.
  • Replace the professional judgement of an engineer, architect, building surveyor, quantity surveyor or licensed tradesperson.
  • Account for site-specific conditions (soil, wind exposure, snow load, seismic zone) unless you explicitly input those parameters.
  • Account for material tolerances, waste allowances, cutting losses or installation overlaps — these must be added by you or your estimator.
  • Provide any warranty, certification or approval that would be recognized by a building authority or insurer.

4.3 Your responsibility

You are solely responsible for verifying every result before it is applied to a real project. Verification means — at minimum — confirming that the formula is appropriate for your use case, checking the result against a second source (a reference table, a manual calculation, a manufacturer's published data), and, for anything involving structural, fire, waterproofing or life-safety consequences, having a licensed professional review the outcome before construction begins.

4.4 When to consult a professional

Consult a licensed professional if your project involves any of the following:

  • Load-bearing elements or any modification to them
  • Roof structures, truss specifications or uplift restraint
  • Foundations, retaining walls or soil-bearing assumptions
  • Fire separation, escape routes or fire-rated assemblies
  • Waterproofing, drainage or damp-proofing
  • Any decision that a building inspector or insurer could reasonably question

Critical

A calculator result is a number. A compliant, buildable, insurable outcome requires the judgement of a professional who has seen your site. Never use a calculator result as the sole basis for a structural, fire, waterproofing or life-safety decision.
