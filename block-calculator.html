Concrete Masonry Units (CMUs), commonly known as cinder blocks or concrete blocks, are the backbone of modern structural walls. From foundations and retaining walls to commercial buildings, CMUs offer unmatched strength, fire resistance, and durability. But unlike brick, which is small and easy to estimate, CMUs are large, heavy, and expensive—making accurate estimation critical.

The most common question we hear from contractors and DIYers is: "How many cinder blocks do I need for my wall?" The answer depends on the wall's dimensions, the block size, and the type of mortar joint. This guide walks through the exact math, mortar mixes, and reinforcement requirements used by professional masons in the US, UK, and Australia.

1. The Math: Calculating Blocks

Standard CMU blocks in the US are 8 inches tall, 8 inches thick, and 16 inches long. However, when calculating, you must account for the mortar joint (usually 3/8 inch). This makes the "nominal" size of the block 8" x 8" x 16", but the actual coverage is slightly larger.

A standard 8x8x16 CMU block covers approximately 1.125 square feet of wall area (including the mortar joint).

Formula: Wall Area (sq ft) ÷ Block Coverage (sq ft) = Number of Blocks.
Example: A 20-foot long wall that is 4 feet high has an area of 80 sq ft. 80 ÷ 1.125 = 72 blocks.

Block Size (US) Coverage Weight
8x8x16 (Standard) 1.125 sq ft ~38 lbs
6x8x16 1.125 sq ft ~28 lbs
4x8x16 1.125 sq ft ~20 lbs
12x8x16 (Jumbo) 1.125 sq ft ~55 lbs

Estimator's Rule: Always add 5% to 10% waste. CMU blocks chip easily and break during transport. For walls with many corners, door/window openings, or bond beams, use 10-15% waste.

2. Mortar: Types and Quantities

CMU blocks are held together by mortar, which is a mix of cement, sand, and lime. There are three main types of mortar, each with different strengths:

  • Type M (2500 PSI): Highest strength. Used for foundations, retaining walls, and heavy-load structures.
  • Type S (1800 PSI): The most common for structural walls. Excellent tensile bond and resistance to wind/seismic loads.
  • Type N (750 PSI): Medium strength. Used for interior walls and non-load-bearing partitions.

Mortar Quantity: A standard 80lb bag of pre-mixed mortar will lay approximately 12 to 15 blocks. This varies with the thickness of the mortar joint and the mason's technique. For larger blocks or thicker joints, you may only lay 10 blocks per bag.

3. Wall Ties and Reinforcement

For structural walls, especially cavity walls (two layers of block with a gap), wall ties are mandatory. They connect the two wythes (layers) and provide lateral stability.

  • US Standard: Corrugated steel ties or adjustable anchors placed every 16 inches horizontally and every 16 inches vertically (every 2nd course).
  • UK Standard: Specific wall ties are dictated by BS EN 845. Ties are spaced every 2nd course and at a maximum of 900mm horizontally.
  • AU Standard: Wall ties follow AS 3700, with specific requirements for cyclonic and non-cyclonic zones.

Rebar: If your wall is reinforced (common in foundations and retaining walls), horizontal rebar (#4 or #5) is laid in the mortar joints every 2 or 3 courses, and vertical rebar is placed in the hollow cores (which are then filled with grout).

4. Core Fill (Grout) Calculation

Hollow CMU blocks are often "core filled" with grout (a high-slump concrete mix) to increase their strength. This is required for:

  • Reinforced walls (vertical rebar).
  • Load-bearing walls (foundations, piers).
  • Walls in seismic zones.

A standard 8x8x16 block has two cores, each with a volume of about 0.22 cubic feet. If you fully grout the wall, you need about 0.44 cubic feet of grout per block. Grout is ordered in cubic yards (US) or cubic meters (Metric).

5. Regional Block Standards (US vs UK vs AU)

While the math for estimating blocks is universal, the way blocks are sized, sold, and reinforced varies significantly across the globe.

United States (ASTM C90)

The US uses "nominal" block sizes: 8" x 8" x 16". The actual dimensions are slightly smaller (7-5/8" x 7-5/8" x 15-5/8") to account for the 3/8" mortar joint. The standard for load-bearing concrete masonry is ASTM C90. The US relies heavily on grout-filled cells for structural strength, especially in high-wind and seismic zones.

United Kingdom (BS EN 771-3)

The UK uses metric blocks, typically 440mm x 215mm x 100mm (or 140mm for cavity walls). The UK standard for aggregate concrete blocks is BS EN 771-3. UK construction often uses "fair-faced" blocks (left exposed) or blocks that are rendered (plastered). Unlike the US, UK block walls often use a "cavity" design with two layers and insulation in between.

Australia (AS 3700)

Australian block sizes are similar to the UK (390mm x 190mm x 190mm is common). Australian standards are dictated by AS 3700 (Masonry Structures). Australia emphasizes "articulated" masonry, using control joints and slip joints to accommodate thermal movement. In cyclonic regions (Queensland, NT), blocks must be reinforced and core-filled to withstand extreme wind uplift.

6. Single Wythe vs. Double Wythe Walls

The wall type dictates the number of blocks required:

  • Single Wythe (US): One layer of 8" blocks. Common for foundations, garden walls, and commercial buildings.
  • Double Wythe (Cavity Wall - UK/AU): Two layers of blocks separated by a 50-100mm cavity. The cavity can hold insulation and provides a break for water penetration. Double wythe walls require twice as many blocks (plus wall ties).

7. Cost Estimation

Block walls are one of the most expensive building methods per square foot, but they are incredibly durable and require little maintenance.

Material Costs

  • US (Standard 8x8x16): $2.50 to $4.00 per block. (Bulk delivery is cheaper).
  • US (Mortar): $8 to $12 per 80lb bag.
  • UK (Standard Block): £1.50 to £3.50 per block (often sold as "dense blocks").
  • AU (Standard Block): AUD $4.00 to $7.00 per block.
  • Rebar & Grout: $15 to $30 per cubic foot of grout (US). Rebar adds $0.50 to $1.00 per sq ft.

Labor Costs

Laying blocks is backbreaking work and requires skill to keep the wall plumb and level.

  • US: $8 to $15 per square foot (installed).
  • UK: £40 to £70 per square meter (installed).
  • Australia: AUD $80 to $150 per square meter (installed).

8. Common Block Wall Mistakes

Avoid these critical errors to ensure your wall is safe and durable:

  1. Poor Footing: Block walls are heavy. The footing must be designed by an engineer, dug below the frost line, and reinforced with rebar. A footing that is too thin will crack under the weight of the wall.
  2. Incorrect Mortar Mix: Using too much cement makes the mortar stronger than the block, causing the block to crack instead of the joint. Always use Type S for structural walls.
  3. No Wall Ties: In double wythe walls, missing wall ties will cause the outer layer to collapse.
  4. Not Plumb: The wall must be checked with a level after every course. A 1-inch lean over an 8-foot wall can fail a building inspection.
  5. Forgetting Control Joints: Masonry expands and contracts. In long walls (over 40 feet), you must install control joints to prevent cracking.

By using our calculator to precisely estimate your blocks, mortar, and grout, and by following proper footing and reinforcement practices, you can build a strong, durable block wall that will last for decades.

Block Wall Questions Answered

Common questions about CMU blocks, mortar, and structural walls.

01 How many cinder blocks do I need for a 10x10 wall?
A 10x10 foot wall has an area of 100 square feet. Using standard 8x8x16 blocks (which cover 1.125 sq ft each), you need about 89 blocks. Add a 5-10% waste factor for cuts and breakage, bringing the total order to 94 to 98 blocks.
02 How many bags of mortar do I need for 100 blocks?
For 100 standard 8x8x16 blocks, you will need approximately 7 to 9 bags of pre-mixed mortar (80lb bags). This assumes a standard 3/8 inch mortar joint. If you are using a thinner joint or a different block size, the bag count will change.
03 What is the best mortar mix for a cinder block wall?
Use Type S mortar for most structural block walls. It has a compressive strength of 1800 PSI and excellent tensile bond, which is crucial for resisting wind and seismic loads. Use Type M for foundations and retaining walls, and Type N for non-load-bearing interior partitions. If you are comparing brick and block, see our Brick Calculator.
04 Do I need to fill cinder blocks with concrete?
It depends on the wall. For load-bearing walls, walls in seismic zones, or walls with vertical rebar, the blocks must be filled with grout (concrete). For a simple garden wall or a low retaining wall, hollow blocks may be sufficient. Always check with a structural engineer if you are unsure.
05 What is the difference between a block and a cinder block?
Traditionally, "cinder block" referred to blocks made with cinders (coal ash), which were lighter but weaker. Modern blocks are made from crushed stone and sand (Concrete Masonry Units or CMU), but the term "cinder block" stuck. So today, they are essentially the same thing. The standard size is 8x8x16 inches.
06 How tall can I build a cinder block wall without reinforcement?
As a general rule, a non-reinforced (hollow) block wall should not exceed 3 to 4 feet in height. Anything taller requires vertical rebar and grout to resist overturning (especially from wind or soil pressure). For retaining walls, any wall over 3 feet usually requires engineered reinforcement.
07 How much does a cinder block wall cost?
In the US, a block wall costs between $8 and $15 per square foot (installed). This includes the blocks, mortar, footing, and labor. In the UK, expect to pay £40 to £70 per sq meter. In Australia, it ranges from AUD $80 to $150 per sq meter. Structural reinforcement (rebar and grout) adds $2-$5 per square foot.
