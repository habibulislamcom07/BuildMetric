The Fundamentals of Masonry Estimation

Masonry is one of the oldest and most durable building methods known to mankind. It involves laying individual units—such as clay bricks, concrete blocks, or natural stone—in a bed of mortar. Because each unit is relatively small, a masonry wall is made up of thousands of components. This makes precise estimation critical. Ordering too few materials will halt your project, while over-ordering leads to thousands of dollars wasted on unused materials.

To accurately use a masonry calculator, you must understand the dimensions of the units, the thickness of the mortar joints, and the bond pattern being used.

Types of Bricks and Their Coverage

Bricks come in various standard sizes depending on the region. In the US, the American Society for Testing and Materials (ASTM) establishes standard dimensions. The most common brick types are:

Brick Type Nominal Dimensions (L x H x W) Bricks per Sq. Ft. Bricks per Sq. M.
Modular 7 5/8" x 2 1/4" x 3 5/8" 6.86 73.8
Queen 7 5/8" x 2 3/4" x 3 1/8" 5.76 62.0
King 9 5/8" x 2 3/4" x 3" 4.5 48.4
Roman 11 5/8" x 1 5/8" x 3 5/8" 6.0 64.6

Note: These calculations assume a standard 3/8-inch mortar joint. Always add a 5-10% waste factor for breakage and cuts.

Understanding Concrete Masonry Units (CMU)

Concrete Masonry Units (CMUs), commonly called cinder blocks or concrete blocks, are the backbone of commercial and foundation construction. Unlike bricks, CMUs are large and hollow. The most common nominal size is 8" x 8" x 16".

Because of their size, a standard CMU block covers 8" x 16" of wall area, which is 128 square inches or 1.125 blocks per square foot. The hollow cores (usually two per block) are designed to allow the insertion of vertical rebar and to be filled with concrete grout, creating a reinforced, load-bearing wall.

Mortar Joints and ASTM C270

The mortar is the glue that holds the masonry together, but it also serves to accommodate slight movements and seal the wall against weather. The standard mortar joint thickness is 3/8 inch.

Mortar mix designs are strictly governed by ASTM C270. The two most common mixes used in residential and light commercial construction are:

  • Type N Mortar (1:1:6): A mix of 1 part Portland cement, 1 part hydrated lime, and 6 parts sand. It has a compressive strength of 750 psi. It is highly recommended for exterior, above-grade load-bearing walls due to its excellent workability and moderate strength.
  • Type S Mortar (1:1/2:4.5): A mix of 1 part cement, 1/2 part lime, and 4.5 parts sand. It has a compressive strength of 1,800 psi. It is used for structural walls, foundation walls, and areas subject to high wind or seismic loads.

Choosing the wrong mortar can be disastrous. Using a mortar that is stronger than the brick can cause the brick to crack under thermal movement, rather than the mortar failing gracefully.

Masonry Bond Patterns and Structural Integrity

The way bricks or blocks are arranged is called the "bond pattern." The bond pattern is not just for aesthetics; it directly affects the structural integrity, load distribution, and weather resistance of the wall. When estimating material, the bond pattern determines the number of cut bricks required at corners and edges.

  • Running Bond: The most common pattern, where each course is offset by half a brick from the course below. It provides excellent strength, distributes loads evenly, and requires very few cuts.
  • Stack Bond: Bricks are stacked directly on top of each other with continuous vertical joints. This is aesthetically striking but structurally weak because it lacks the overlapping (interlocking) of units. Stack bond requires horizontal joint reinforcement (truss-type wires) to prevent cracking.
  • Flemish Bond: Alternating headers (short ends) and stretchers (long ends) in the same course. It is highly decorative and historically popular but requires more labor and complex corner detailing.

Wall Bracing and Code Requirements (TMS 402)

Masonry walls are heavy, but they are also brittle. They must be designed to resist lateral loads from wind and seismic activity. In the US, the International Building Code (IBC) references TMS 402 (Building Code Requirements for Masonry Structures) for these design criteria.

A critical concept in masonry is slenderness ratio. A tall, thin wall can buckle under wind loads if it is not properly braced. To prevent this:

  • Walls must be laterally supported by intersecting walls, floors, or roofs at specific intervals.
  • For cavity walls (two wythes of brick with a gap between them), wall ties must be used to connect the outer and inner leaves. Ties are typically spaced at a maximum of 24 inches horizontally and 16 inches vertically.
  • Structural reinforcement (rebar) inside grouted CMU cores is mandatory for load-bearing walls in high-wind or seismic zones.
Pro Tip: Always use a weep hole at the base of the wall (above the flashing) in brick veneer walls. This allows trapped moisture to escape and prevents efflorescence and spalling (freeze-thaw damage) in cold climates.

Calculating Waste Allowance for Masonry

Masonry units are heavy and brittle. Dropping a pallet of bricks or cutting corners incorrectly means you will break a percentage of them. Standard industry practice dictates adding a 5% to 10% waste factor to your total material order.

For standard running bond walls with minimal corners, a 5% allowance is usually sufficient. However, if your wall includes:

  • Complex architectural features (curved walls, arches).
  • Numerous window and door openings requiring lintels and cut bricks.
  • Stack bond patterns requiring precise cuts.

...then increase your waste allowance to 10%. Using our masonry calculators automatically applies an adjustable waste slider, ensuring you never under-order.

International Masonry Standards (UK & AU)

Just like framing, masonry codes vary globally. In the UK and Europe, masonry is governed by Eurocode 6 (BS EN 1996). UK bricks are often metric (e.g., 215mm x 65mm x 102.5mm) and mortar joints are typically 10mm. In Australia, the standard is AS 3700 (Masonry Structures), with clay bricks commonly measuring 230mm x 76mm x 110mm. Always verify your calculator settings match your region's metric or imperial standards.

Frequently Asked Questions

Expert answers to the most common masonry and material estimation questions.

What is masonry in construction?
Masonry is the building of structures from individual units, typically laid in courses and bound together by mortar. Common materials include clay bricks, concrete blocks (CMU), stone, and glass blocks. Masonry is known for its durability, fire resistance, and thermal mass, and is governed by codes like TMS 402/602 in the US and BS EN 1996 (Eurocode 6).
What is the standard mortar mix ratio for bricks?
The most common mortar for brick is Type N, which is a 1:1:6 mix of Portland cement, hydrated lime, and sand. Type N is recommended for general-purpose exterior and interior load-bearing walls. For softer historic bricks, Type O (1:2:9) is preferred to allow flexibility. Always refer to ASTM C270 for mortar specifications.
How do I calculate the number of bricks per square foot?
For a standard modular brick (7 5/8 x 3 5/8 x 2 1/4 inches) laid in a running bond with 3/8-inch mortar joints, you need approximately 6.86 bricks per square foot of wall area. For a queen size brick, it's about 5.7 bricks per square foot, and for a king size, about 4.5. Always add a 5-10% waste factor.
What is the difference between brick and CMU block?
Bricks are typically smaller, clay-fired units used for veneer, facades, or solid walls. CMU (Concrete Masonry Unit) blocks are much larger, hollow concrete blocks (usually 8x8x16 inches) used primarily for structural walls, foundations, and partitions. CMUs are faster to lay due to their size and are often reinforced with rebar and grout.
How do you calculate mortar for a brick wall?
Mortar is calculated by volume. For a standard modular brick wall, the mortar volume is roughly 0.20 cubic feet per square foot of wall. A typical 80lb bag of premixed mortar yields about 0.7 cubic feet. Alternatively, for a 1:1:6 mix, you will need approximately 1 part cement, 1 part lime, and 6 parts sand by volume, plus water.
What are the common masonry building codes?
In the US, masonry is governed by the International Building Code (IBC) which references TMS 402 (Building Code Requirements for Masonry Structures) and TMS 602 (Specification for Masonry Structures). In the UK and Europe, Eurocode 6 (BS EN 1996) applies. In Australia, AS 3700 covers masonry structures. Local codes may impose additional seismic or wind requirements.
How much does a masonry wall cost per square foot?
Cost varies widely by material and region. In the US, a brick veneer wall costs roughly $10 to $20 per square foot installed, while a structural CMU block wall costs about $8 to $15 per square foot. Stone veneer can run $20 to $40+ per square foot. Labor typically accounts for 50-60% of the total cost.
