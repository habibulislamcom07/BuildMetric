The Ultimate Guide to Masonry Estimation

Masonry is one of the oldest and most durable building methods, but estimating materials requires precision. Whether you are building a brick boundary wall in the UK, a concrete block foundation in the US, or a retaining wall in Australia, our calculators account for mortar joint thickness, block sizes, and waste. Pro Tip: Always order 5% to 10% extra bricks or blocks to account for breakage and cuts around corners.

Understanding Mortar Mix and Joints

Bricks and blocks are only as strong as the mortar that binds them. The mortar mix is a combination of cement, sand, and water. The ratio of these ingredients determines the strength and flexibility of the wall.

Common Mortar Mix Ratios

  • 1:3 (Cement : Sand): High-strength mix used for load-bearing walls, below-ground masonry, and harsh weather conditions. It is very strong but less flexible.
  • 1:4 or 1:5: The standard general-purpose mix for above-ground brick and block walls. It offers a good balance of strength and flexibility.
  • 1:6: Weaker mix used for internal partition walls or non-load-bearing work. It is more flexible and easier to work with but should not be used for structural walls.

The mortar joint (the gap between bricks) is typically 10mm (3/8 inch) thick. This joint thickness is critical for the calculation. If you assume the bricks are 215mm long but ignore the 10mm mortar joint, you will significantly overestimate the number of bricks needed. Our Brick Calculator automatically accounts for mortar joint thickness.

The Role of Wall Ties

In cavity wall construction (common in the UK), the inner leaf (blockwork) and outer leaf (brickwork) are connected by steel wall ties. These ties provide structural stability and prevent water from crossing the cavity. Ties are typically installed at a rate of 2.5 per square meter (or 1 per 0.4 sq m).

You must use the correct type of tie for your application. For standard residential walls, a stainless steel double-triangle tie is common. In coastal areas, you must use higher-grade stainless steel to resist corrosion.

Global Brick and Block Sizes

One of the biggest challenges in international construction is that brick and block sizes vary significantly around the world. If you use a US brick size calculation for a UK project, you will end up with the wrong quantity.

USA

In the US, bricks are typically modular or standard. A standard US brick is 3-5/8" x 2-1/4" x 8" with a 3/8" mortar joint. This yields approximately 6.86 bricks per square foot of wall area. Concrete blocks (CMUs) are typically 8" x 8" x 16", yielding 1.125 blocks per square foot.

UK

In the UK, the standard brick size is 215mm x 65mm x 102.5mm. With a 10mm mortar joint, this yields exactly 60 bricks per square meter. Concrete blocks are typically 440mm x 215mm x 100mm, yielding 10 blocks per square meter. Our Block Calculator supports both UK and US sizes.

Australia

In Australia, standard bricks are 230mm x 76mm x 110mm. With a 10mm joint, this yields approximately 48.5 bricks per square meter. Concrete blocks are typically 390mm x 190mm x 190mm, yielding 12.5 blocks per square meter.

Retaining Walls: Structural Engineering and Drainage

Retaining walls are a completely different beast compared to standard brick or block walls. Their primary job is to hold back soil, which means they must resist immense lateral earth pressure and hydrostatic pressure from water.

1. The Critical Role of Drainage

The number one cause of retaining wall failure is poor drainage. When soil becomes saturated, its weight and pressure increase dramatically. Every retaining wall must have a drainage system behind it. This typically consists of a layer of clean gravel (washed stone) and a perforated drainage pipe (French drain) at the base.

Additionally, the wall itself must be permeable or have weep holes to allow water to escape. Using a geotextile fabric between the soil and gravel prevents the drain from clogging with fine soil particles. Our Retaining Wall Calculator helps you estimate the gravel and block quantities required.

2. Battered Walls (The Lean-Back)

In traditional masonry retaining walls, the wall is built with a slight "batter" (a lean back toward the soil). This shifts the center of gravity of the wall, making it more stable against overturning. The wall also needs a substantial concrete footing that extends below the frost line.

3. Geogrid Reinforcement

For walls higher than 3 to 4 feet, a simple gravity wall may not be enough. Modern segmental retaining walls use geogrid—a strong plastic mesh laid horizontally between the block courses and extending back into the soil. This reinforces the soil mass, essentially turning the soil into a larger, stable block that holds the wall in place.

Foundations for Masonry Walls

Brick and block walls are incredibly heavy. A standard 100mm concrete block wall weighs roughly 150 kg per square meter (or 30 lbs per square foot). This load must be transferred to stable ground.

The foundation (footing) for a masonry wall must be wider than the wall itself, typically extending 100mm to 200mm (4 to 8 inches) beyond each side. It must be poured using concrete (typically 2500 to 3000 PSI) and must extend below the local frost line to prevent frost heave from cracking the wall. For calculating the concrete volume for your foundation, use our Concrete Calculator.

Weathering and Efflorescence

If you have ever seen a white, powdery stain on a brick wall, you have seen efflorescence. This is caused by water-soluble salts migrating to the surface of the masonry and crystallizing as the water evaporates.

While usually not structurally damaging, it is unsightly. The best way to prevent it is to ensure good detailing (proper flashing, weepholes, and copings) to prevent water from entering the masonry in the first place. Applying a breathable water repellent after the wall has cured can also help.

Frequently Asked Questions

Masonry Calculator FAQs

Direct answers to common questions about bricks, blocks, and mortar.

01

How many bricks do I need for a wall?
For a standard UK brick (215 x 65mm) with a 10mm mortar joint, you need approximately 60 bricks per square meter. For a standard US modular brick (3-5/8 x 2-1/4 inches) with a 3/8 inch joint, you need about 6.86 bricks per square foot. Use our Brick Calculator for exact counts.
02

What is the correct mortar mix for a brick wall?
For general-purpose brickwork, a 1:4 or 1:5 cement-to-sand mix is standard. For load-bearing walls, a stronger 1:3 mix is often specified. Using the wrong mix can lead to weak walls or excessive shrinkage cracking.
03

How many concrete blocks are in a square meter?
A standard UK concrete block (440 x 215mm) with a 10mm joint covers approximately 0.1 square meters, meaning you need roughly 10 blocks per square meter. In the US, a standard 8x8x16 inch CMU covers 0.89 square feet, meaning 1.125 blocks per square foot. Use our Block Calculator for exact counts.
04

Do I need wall ties for a cavity wall?
Yes. If you are building a cavity wall (two leaves of masonry with a gap between), you must use wall ties to connect the inner and outer leaves. Ties are typically spaced at 900mm horizontally and 450mm vertically.
05

How much does brickwork cost per square meter?
In the UK, a standard brick wall costs roughly £60 to £100 per square meter for materials and labor. In the US, it costs $10 to $20 per square foot. In Australia, expect $80 to $150 AUD per square meter. For full project estimates, use our Construction Cost Calculator.
06

What is the difference between a brick and a block?
Bricks are generally smaller (fits in one hand) and are used for facing walls or decorative work. Blocks are larger (usually concrete or cinder) and are used for structural walls or foundations. Blocks are cheaper but less aesthetically pleasing. Use our Block Calculator for structural walls.
07

Do I need a foundation for a brick wall?
Yes. Brick and block walls are heavy, so they require a concrete footing (foundation) that is wider than the wall itself. The foundation must extend below the frost line in cold climates. Never build a masonry wall directly on dirt. Use our Concrete Calculator to estimate the footing volume.
