Our Story

Why BuildMetric Exists

BuildMetric started — like many bad ideas that turn out to be good ones — with an argument on a building site. It was a Friday afternoon on a residential roof replacement in the north of England. The drawings specified 30°. The roofer had been told "roughly 7 in 12." A subcontractor had written "6:12" on the back of a delivery note. Three people, one roof, three numbers, and every one of them was being used as if it meant the same thing. It didn't. The material order was short. The ridge board had to be re-cut. And a four-hour conversation about conversion factors followed.

That conversation stayed with us. Not because the numbers were difficult — they are trivial trigonometry — but because the same numbers mean different things to different trades in different countries, and there was no single, well-explained tool that reconciled them. Existing calculators were either marketing funnels for material suppliers, hidden behind paywalls, or written by someone who clearly had never held a nail gun. Some returned answers without explaining the formula. Others explained the formula but used the wrong one. A few confidently mixed slope percentage with rafter multiplier — a mistake we estimate inflates material orders on thousands of projects every year.

BuildMetric was built to be the resource we could not find. It began as a single roof pitch calculator, written on a laptop between site visits, and grew — slowly, deliberately — into a library of construction calculators covering concrete volumes, timber framing, paint coverage, drywall estimation and other everyday estimating tasks. Every tool had to pass the same three tests before publication: the formula had to be derivable from first principles, the output had to be verifiable against a known reference case, and the surrounding explanation had to be written in plain language that a competent tradesperson could follow without a maths degree.

We are not a software company that happens to write about construction. We are construction people — quantity surveyors, structural engineers, licensed roofing contractors — who happen to have learned to build web tools. That distinction matters. It means our default question is not "what feature can we add?" but "what would actually help someone standing on a scaffold with a tape measure and a phone?" It means we test our tools against the jobs we have worked on ourselves. And it means we are comfortable saying, often and explicitly, that a calculator is not a substitute for professional judgement — because we have all seen what happens when it is treated as one.

We publish for three markets — the United States, the United Kingdom and Australia — because those are the three regulatory environments our team knows intimately. We do not pretend to cover every jurisdiction. Where codes, units and standards differ, we say so explicitly rather than flattening the differences into a single "international" answer. A roof pitch that is compliant in Florida may need a different underlay in the Scottish Highlands, and a timber frame that passes AS 1684 in Queensland may not pass an equivalent check under IRC in Minnesota. Our calculators apply universal mathematics; the surrounding guides explain the local context that mathematics has to be applied in.

The name "BuildMetric" was chosen because it captures both halves of what we do: the act of building, and the discipline of measuring it precisely. It is not a promise that every answer will be perfect. It is a promise that every answer will be measurable, explainable and open to correction. If you find that we have fallen short of that promise — if a formula is wrong, a unit is confused, a code reference is out of date — we want to hear from you. Corrections are how the library gets better. And on a site that is defined by its willingness to publish honest numbers, being open to correction is not weakness; it is the whole point.

If a calculator cannot explain its formula in one sentence, it should not be publishing a number.

How We Build Trust

Our Editorial Process — Step by Step

Every calculator and guide passes through five review stages before it is published. Nothing bypasses them — not even small updates.

  1. 01

    Draft

    A topic is drafted based on real field questions and reference to the current code edition.

  2. 02

    Technical Review

    A practising professional verifies the formula, worked examples and code references.

  3. 03

    Plain-Language Edit

    A technical editor rewrites for clarity — no jargon, no maths-heavy tangents, no fluff.

  4. 04

    Verification

    Tool is tested against known reference cases in imperial and metric. Output must match.

  5. 05

    Publish & Date

    Published with a visible version number and date. Reviewed again each year.

Every Page Has an Owner

Each calculator and guide has a named reviewer, a visible version number and an update date. When something changes — whether a formula is corrected, a code reference is updated, or a new unit is added — that change is logged publicly in the page's history.

Transparency

How We Fund BuildMetric

Every calculator and guide on this site is free, and will stay free. Here is exactly how that works — no hidden funnels, no gated results, no dark patterns.

Display Advertising

Primary

We run a small number of clearly marked display ad slots served by third-party ad networks. Ads are labelled and never overlay the calculator results. We do not serve pop-ups, auto-playing video, or interstitial ads that block reading.

  • Clearly marked "Ad Space" placeholders.
  • No ads inside result boxes or calculator UI.
  • Advertisers have no influence on formula or editorial choices.

Affiliate Links

Secondary

Where our guides reference specific products — a particular membrane, a tool, a material sample — we may include an affiliate link to a supplier. If you buy through that link, we receive a small commission at no extra cost to you.

  • Disclosed at the top of any page with affiliate links.
  • Commission rate never affects product placement or recommendation.
  • No product is recommended solely because of affiliate revenue.

Red Line

What We Will Never Do

  • Sell calculator outputs or lead data. Your inputs and results are not sold.
  • Accept paid reviews. No payment, sponsorship or affiliate relationship changes our editorial conclusions.
  • Paywall tools or results. No sign-up wall, no forced newsletter, no gated "premium" calculation.
  • Sell sponsored rankings. Search result positions and internal "related tools" lists are algorithm-based, not paid.

Why Only Three Markets?

It would be easy to claim "global coverage" and paste a flag emoji onto a comparison table. It would also be dishonest. We cover three markets because our team has worked extensively in all three — surveyed in the UK, framed in the US, and reviewed Australian code on project sites. If we added a fourth market tomorrow, we would have to either hire someone with genuine local expertise or accept a lower standard of accuracy. We are not willing to do either.

Users outside those three markets are still welcome to use our calculators — the underlying mathematics is universal. What we cannot do is provide regional code context, local approval guidance or jurisdiction-specific material recommendations. For that, you need a professional licensed where you are building.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common Questions About BuildMetric

Seven direct answers about who we are, how we fund the site, how we verify our calculators and how corrections are handled.

01 Who owns and runs BuildMetric?

BuildMetric is independently owned and operated. It is not backed by a manufacturer, a supplier, a lead-generation network or a venture-capital fund. It is run by a small editorial and engineering team — quantity surveyors, structural engineers, licensed roofing contractors and technical editors — with combined field experience across the United States, United Kingdom and Australia. The team writes, reviews, publishes and maintains every calculator and guide on the site.

02 Are BuildMetric calculators really free to use?

Yes. Every calculator and written guide on buildmetric.org is free to use, with no paywall, no forced sign-up and no gated results. The site is funded through clearly marked display advertising and, on some product references, affiliate commissions. Advertising partners and affiliates have no influence over calculator formulas, editorial conclusions or rankings. There are no "premium" tiers, no trials and no hidden paywalls.

03 How do you verify the accuracy of your calculators?

Every calculator is built from formulas that can be derived from first principles, and is tested against a set of known reference cases in both imperial and metric before publication. Each tool is reviewed by at least one practising professional — an engineer, quantity surveyor or licensed contractor — and periodically re-reviewed against current code editions including IRC 2021, BS 5534 and AS 1684. When a user reports an error, we reproduce the issue, test the fix and publish the change with a visible version number and update date.

04 Why do you only cover the USA, UK and Australia?

Because our team has genuine field experience in those three markets — surveying in the UK, framing in the US and reviewing Australian code on project sites. The underlying mathematics is universal, so users anywhere can use our calculators. What we cannot honestly provide outside those three markets is regional code context, local approval guidance or jurisdiction-specific material recommendations. Rather than claim "global coverage" and deliver a lower standard, we have chosen to be explicit about the three markets we know and cover them properly.

05 Do you accept sponsored content, paid reviews or paid placements?

No. We do not accept paid reviews, sponsored editorials, paid "guest posts" or SEO link-insertion offers. Advertisers can purchase display placements, but they cannot purchase editorial coverage, alter calculator formulas, influence rankings or preview drafts before publication. We occasionally accept non-paid guest contributions from practising professionals with verifiable credentials and field experience, and those are reviewed under the same standards as our own content. Any sponsorship or affiliate relationship is clearly disclosed on the page where it appears.

06 Can I contribute a calculator, guide or guest article?

We occasionally accept guest contributions from practising professionals — engineers, surveyors, architects, licensed contractors — who can demonstrate verifiable credentials and relevant field experience. We do not accept paid placements, sponsored guest posts or SEO link-insertion offers, and we do not publish AI-generated content without a named human reviewer. If you believe your contribution meets our standards, email contact@buildmetric.org with your credentials, a short writing sample and a proposed topic. We respond to accepted pitches only.

07 How do you handle corrections to published content?

Every valid correction is logged, tested and published with a visible version number and update date on the affected page. Reported issues are triaged within 48 hours and resolved within two business days, and we credit the reporter (with permission) in the page's update history. Over 250 user-reported fixes have been validated and published since launch in 2023. Corrections are how the library gets better — we would much rather be corrected than be quietly wrong.
