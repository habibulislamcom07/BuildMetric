// WHY WE EXIST //

Most construction calculators online are either toys or traps.

A toy gives you a number with no formula shown, no units enforced, and no standard cited. Type feet into a field expecting inches and it happily returns nonsense. A trap hides the number behind a signup wall, then sells your email to three suppliers who call you for a week. Both are common. Neither is what a tradesperson needs at 6:40 am with a pour scheduled for 8.

BuildMetric was built because the arithmetic of construction is not complicated — it is unforgiving. A slab that is 0.4 m³ short stops a pour. A footing that is 15% under-designed fails inspection. A roof with the wrong pitch drains water into a wall and rots a header three winters later. Getting the number right is the entire job. Every calculator on this site exists to make that faster and less error-prone.

Three commitments we will not break

One: show the formula. Every result is accompanied by the equation that produced it, the units it expects, and the standard it follows. If a calculation is region-dependent — concrete cover, rebar lap length, wire gauge ampacity, snow load — the applicable code is named and can be overridden. You should never have to trust a black box on a job site.

Two: no signup, no email wall, no upsell. The calculators run entirely in the browser. Nothing you type is sent to a server. Saved results live in localStorage on your device. You can export a PDF without giving us anything. The only place we ask for contact information is the supplier-quote form, and that is opt-in, clearly labelled, and never required to use a tool.

Three: work in the units and standards you actually use. A Canadian framer works in feet, inches, and fractions, and follows CSA A23.3 for concrete. An Indian site engineer works in metres, millimetres, and IS 456. A German crew works entirely in Eurocode. The same tool adapts to all three without a language barrier or a unit conversion mistake. Region is auto-detected, cached, and overridable in one click.

What we are not

We are not a design service. These calculators compute quantities, spans, volumes, loads, and costs from inputs you provide. They do not replace structural engineering, licensed trade judgement, or a stamped set of drawings. Where a calculation touches structural safety — beam sizing, column loads, footing bearing, anchor pull-out — the result is clearly marked as preliminary and the tool links to the governing code clause so you or your engineer can verify. We would rather be useful and honest than useful and dangerous.

We are also not a marketplace. There are no affiliate links, no paid placements inside results, and no sponsored tools. Where a supplier form appears, it is labelled and it is optional. The calculators come first. Advertising keeps the lights on; it never enters the numbers.

Built for the field, not the boardroom

BuildMetric pages are tuned to load in under 1.5 seconds on a mid-range phone over 4G, and to keep working after the first load even with no signal — because basements, tunnels, and rural job sites do not have reliable connectivity. Every input control is at least 44 pixels tall so it can be tapped with a gloved thumb. Every number is set in a monospaced font so digits line up and misread zeros are less likely. Nothing plays automatically, nothing pops up, and nothing moves unless you asked it to.

That is the whole product. Numbers you can trust, at the speed you need them, in the units you already use. Everything else on this site is in service of that.

// WHY TRUST US //

Six reasons the numbers hold up.

No fake badges. No invented reviews. Just the verifiable things we do differently.

  • Formulas on every page

    Each tool displays its equation in a syntax-highlighted code block, with the standard it derives from. If you disagree with the method, you can see exactly what it does.

  • Region-aware standards

    The code that governs your numbers changes with location. ACI 318-19, BS EN 1992, AS 3600, CSA A23.3, IS 456, NZS 3101, SANS 10100, and Eurocode are all built in and switch automatically.

  • Worked examples in US & metric

    Every tool includes at least one fully worked example in both imperial and metric units, so you can check the arithmetic against your own project before you rely on it.

  • No hidden data collection

    Calculator inputs never leave your browser. There is no account, no tracking of values, and no third-party analytics on form fields. The only data we ever receive is what you explicitly submit on the supplier form.

  • Waste factors you control

    Real jobs lose material to cutting, breakage, spillage, and over-excavation. Waste is togglable per tool with sensible per-trade defaults (5–15%) — never hidden inside the number.

  • Fast enough to use on site

    Pages are target-built for under 1.5 seconds on 4G. Calculators update as you type with a 250 ms debounce. No spinners, no loading states, no waiting — because a job-site question needs an answer now.

// STANDARDS WE FOLLOW //

Nine regional codes. One interface.

BuildMetric ships with the codes that govern concrete cover, rebar development length, wind and snow loads, wire ampacity, duct sizing, and pipe capacity across the major English-speaking and European markets. Region is auto-detected and overridable.

  • US

    ACI 318-19

    Building Code Requirements for Structural Concrete. Drives cover, lap length, and shear checks in the concrete and masonry tools.

  • UK

    BS EN 1992

    Eurocode 2 — Design of Concrete Structures, as adopted in the UK National Annex. Governs cover, anchorage, and durability classes.

  • AU

    AS 3600

    Concrete Structures (Australia). Used for member sizing, deflection limits, and exposure classification across Australian tools.

  • CA

    CSA A23.3

    Design of Concrete Structures (Canada). Governs concrete tools in Canada, including cold-weather placement guidance.

  • IN

    IS 456:2000

    Plain and Reinforced Concrete — Code of Practice (India). Basis for cover, mix proportions, and rebar spacing in Indian tools.

  • EU

    Eurocode

    EN 1990 through EN 1999. Applied for DE and FR regions across concrete, steel, and geotechnical calculators.

NZ → AS/NZS 3600 · ZA → SANS 10100 · AE → BS EN (default) · Regional override available on every tool page.

// FAQ //

Straight answers.

The questions we get most. Nothing padded, nothing dodged.

  • Are BuildMetric calculators free to use? +

    Yes. Every calculator on BuildMetric is free, requires no signup, and shows the formula used. We are supported by clean, non-intrusive advertising and optional B2B supplier connections. There are no paywalls, no trial periods, and no email gates. If a future feature ever changes that, it will be clearly labelled and separate from the core tools.

  • Which construction standards do the calculators follow? +

    Calculators follow the standard relevant to your detected region: ACI 318-19 for USA, BS EN 1992 for UK, AS 3600 for Australia, CSA A23.3 for Canada, IS 456 for India, NZS/AS 3600 for New Zealand, SANS 10100 for South Africa, and Eurocode for Germany and France. The active standard is displayed on every tool page, cited against the specific equation it governs, and can be overridden manually from the geo banner.

  • Do I need to create an account to save results? +

    No account is needed. Saved results are stored locally in your browser using localStorage. You can export any result as a PDF for offline use, or copy a permalink that reopens the same tool with your inputs pre-filled. Nothing is uploaded to our servers, and clearing your browser data clears your saved results — which is the correct privacy trade-off for job-site numbers.

  • How accurate are the material estimates? +

    Calculators use published formulas from recognised standards bodies and material suppliers. Waste factors can be toggled (5–15%, category-dependent) to account for cutting, spillage, and breakage. Results are typically within 3–5% of a manual take-off on straightforward geometry. For structural work — beams, columns, footings, anchors — always have final quantities and member sizes verified by a licensed engineer. The calculators are accurate; they are not a substitute for a stamped drawing.

  • Can I use BuildMetric on a job site with no signal? +

    Core calculator logic runs entirely in the browser. Once a page has loaded, it will continue to work without a network connection. We recommend opening frequently used tool pages while you still have signal — the browser caches CSS, JS, and fonts, so reopening them offline works reliably. Saved results and exports are unaffected by loss of connectivity.
