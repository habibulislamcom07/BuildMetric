      Why BuildMetric Exists

      Built by engineers, for the people who actually pour, frame, wire, and roof.

      Construction estimating has a quiet problem. The math is not hard — it is arithmetic, geometry, and a handful of code tables — but it is fragmented across a dozen sources. A contractor pricing a slab in Texas pulls a thickness from ACI 318, a waste factor from a supplier's paper catalogue, a rebar spacing table from an old design guide, and a per-cubic-yard price from whoever answered the phone that morning. Multiply that by every project, every trade, every jurisdiction, and the small errors compound into real money lost on over-orders, under-orders, and change orders that should never have happened.

      BuildMetric was built to close that gap. We are a team of civil, structural, and MEP engineers who got tired of watching skilled tradespeople rebuild the same spreadsheet for the twentieth time. Every one of our 300+ calculators takes a published engineering standard — ACI 318-19 for concrete, BS EN 1992 for Eurocode, AS 3600 for Australia, IS 456 for India — and turns it into a clean, fast, mobile-friendly tool that gives you a defensible answer in seconds. No login. No paywall. No "download our free PDF" bait-and-switch. Just the calculator, the formula, and the standard it came from.

      We also know that a number alone is not an estimate. That is why every tool page combines three things: the live calculator, a full worked example in both US and metric units, and a Hybrid Mode section where you can layer your own local supplier prices, manual line items, and pro tips on top of the calculated result. The output is not a screenshot you have to transcribe — it is a shareable, printable report you can hand to a client, a supplier, or an inspector without retyping a single figure.

      Geographic accuracy matters more than most platforms admit. A footing that passes inspection in Manchester is not the same footing that passes in Melbourne, and neither is the same as one in Mumbai. BuildMetric detects your country on first load, defaults to the correct units, currency, and code standard for your region, and lets you change any of it with one tap. United States and Canadian visitors get imperial units and ACI or NRC references. UK, Australian, Indian, and Eurozone visitors get metric units with their national code. You never have to convert by hand or guess which table applies.

      BuildMetric stays free because we do not sell your data, we do not require a subscription, and we do not push affiliate products. Revenue comes from unobtrusive display advertising and an optional B2B pathway where verified suppliers can respond to your material requests — that is it. If you are a contractor who wants quotes from three local ready-mix plants, we connect you. If you are a homeowner sizing a solar array on a Saturday afternoon, we give you the number and stay out of your way. Either way, the calculator is the product, and the calculator is free.

      Finally, we believe estimates should be honest about their limits. Every tool page lists the standard it follows, the assumptions it makes, and a clear disclaimer about when a licensed engineer needs to sign off. We would rather tell you "get this reviewed" than hand you a false sense of precision. That is the difference between a calculator that looks impressive and one you can actually build from.

      Standards We Follow

      Every calculator cites the exact published standard it is built on. No black boxes, no made-up formulas.

      🇺🇸

      ACI 318-19

      United States · American Concrete Institute

      Structural concrete design, minimum thickness, reinforcement detailing, and slab-on-grade provisions.

      🇬🇧

      BS EN 1992 / Eurocode 2

      United Kingdom & EU · British Standards Institution

      Design of concrete structures, exposure classes, cover, and reinforcement rules for UK and European builds.

      🇦🇺

      AS 3600 / AS 2870

      Australia & New Zealand · Standards Australia

      Concrete structures and residential slabs & footings, including reactive soil classifications.

      🇮🇳

      IS 456 / IS 10262

      India · Bureau of Indian Standards

      Plain and reinforced concrete code of practice, plus concrete mix proportioning guidelines.

      🇨🇦

      NRC / CSA

      Canada · National Research Council

      National Building Code of Canada and CSA concrete, wood, and structural design references.

      🌍

      ISO 9001 / IEC

      International · Electrical & quality references

      Quality management principles and electrical installation references for our MEP and energy tools.

      Not sure which standard applies? The BuildMetric geo-detection system sets it automatically based on your country — and every tool page lists the exact reference in its Standards Reference section.

      Frequently Asked Questions

      Quick answers about how BuildMetric works, what it costs, and how accurate the results are.

      Is BuildMetric really free to use? +

      Yes. Every one of the 300+ calculators on BuildMetric is completely free with no account, no subscription, and no credit card required. You can open any tool, run any number of estimates, and print or share the results without ever being asked to pay. The platform is funded by non-intrusive display advertising and optional B2B supplier connections, so you never pay for calculator access and we never charge you to see your own results.

      Which construction standards does BuildMetric follow? +

      Our calculators reference ACI 318-19 and ACI 211 for the United States, BS EN 1992 and BS 8500 for the United Kingdom, AS 3600 and AS 2870 for Australia, and IS 456 and IS 10262 for India. Canadian tools reference the National Building Code of Canada and CSA standards, and Eurozone tools reference Eurocode 2. Every tool page lists the exact standard it uses in the Standards Reference section at the bottom, so engineers, contractors, and inspectors can verify the methodology before relying on the output.

      Does BuildMetric work in both metric and imperial units? +

      Yes. BuildMetric automatically detects your country on first visit and defaults to the correct measurement system: imperial (feet, inches, pounds) for the United States and Canada, and metric (metres, centimetres, kilograms) for the United Kingdom, Australia, India, New Zealand, South Africa, the UAE, and the Eurozone. If the detection is wrong or you simply prefer the other system, there is a unit toggle on every calculator that switches all inputs and outputs instantly. Your preference is remembered for 30 days using local storage, so you only set it once.

      Can I use BuildMetric results for a building permit application? +

      BuildMetric results are designed as a professional estimating aid and are suitable for planning, budgeting, supplier ordering, and preliminary design. They are not a substitute for stamped engineering drawings. For any permit application, structural alteration, or load-bearing change, have a licensed engineer or architect in your jurisdiction review the calculations against the locally adopted code. Our Standards Reference section tells you which published code each tool is based on, which makes that review faster and cheaper for your engineer.

      How accurate are BuildMetric's construction calculators? +

      Our formulas are sourced directly from published engineering standards and verified against worked examples from ACI, BS, AS, and IS design guides. Typical accuracy for material quantity take-offs is within 2–5% of actual field quantities when you input accurate dimensions and use the correct code assumptions for your region. For real-world purchasing, always add a waste factor of 5–10% depending on the material and site conditions — every BuildMetric calculator includes a one-tap waste toggle that handles this automatically. Cost outputs depend on local market rates, so treat them as a planning range rather than a firm quote.
