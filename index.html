    // WHY //

    Built by Engineers, Not Marketers.

    BuildMetric started in 2023 as a spreadsheet. A concrete foreman in Texas was tired of watching crews order a yard and a half short on a Friday afternoon because somebody rounded a slab thickness the wrong way. He built a calculator. Then his estimator asked for a rebar version. Then the electrician on the same job asked for a wire-gauge check. Six months later there were forty of them, all sitting in a shared drive with names like slab_v3_FINAL_use_this_one.xlsx. That was the problem BuildMetric was built to solve.

    Every calculator on this site exists because a specific trade asked for it in a specific situation. The concrete calculators follow ACI 318-19 for reinforcement checks and ACI 301 for placement tolerances. The rebar spacing tool uses the clear-cover rules from ACI 318-19 §20.5.1.3, not a rule of thumb. When you switch your region to the UK, the same concrete calculator switches to BS EN 1992-1-1 with the UK National Annex, and the partial safety factors change with it. That is not a cosmetic toggle. The number that comes out is the number a UK engineer would defend.

    We do not use round numbers as defaults. A four-inch slab is 4 inches, not "about 100mm." A 2×10 joist is 1.5 × 9.25 inches of actual section, not the nominal 2×10 that lumber yards sell you. When a formula needs a unit weight — 145 lb/ft³ for normal-weight concrete, 2,400 kg/m³ in metric — we use the value the standard specifies, and we show it to you in the formula block so you can argue with it if you disagree.

    Accuracy here means three things. First, the formula matches the published standard, and we cite the clause. Second, the constants match what material actually weighs and measures on delivery day, not what a spec sheet claims in ideal conditions. Third, the result is given in the units the supplier actually sells in — cubic yards, not cubic feet, in the US; cubic metres to two decimals in the UK and Australia. When a supplier asks "how much do you need?", the answer should be the number on the screen, unchanged.

    We also try to be honest about what a calculator cannot do. A slab calculator cannot tell you whether the subgrade is compacted. A solar sizing tool cannot see the oak tree that shades your roof at 3pm in July. A cost estimator cannot know that your local lumber yard raised prices 8% last Tuesday. Those are site conditions and market conditions, and no formula in any standard covers them. What we can do is give you a defensible baseline, show every assumption that went into it, and let you adjust. That is what a good estimator does anyway.

    BuildMetric is free, and it stays free. There is no pro tier hiding the useful tools, no five-calculator limit before you hit a paywall, and no account required to save your work (though the Save Result button will keep the last calculation in your browser if you want it). We fund the site with display advertising and with optional supplier quote requests for contractors who want to compare material prices in their ZIP. Neither is required to use a single calculator. If the ads ever stop paying for the servers, we will tell you before we change anything. Engineers do not like surprises, and neither do we.

    — The BuildMetric Engineering Team

    // TRUST //

    Why Engineers Trust BuildMetric

    • Standards-Based

      Formulas cite the clause they follow — ACI 318-19 §22.5.1 for shear, BS EN 1992-1-1 Table 3.1 for concrete strength classes, IS 456 Cl. 26 for development length. If a formula cannot cite a clause, it is flagged as a field rule, not a code rule.

    • Engineer-Reviewed

      Every calculator has been cross-checked by a licensed engineer in at least one of our supported regions. The concrete volume tool was verified against 200+ pours logged by three separate ready-mix suppliers in the US and UK.

    • Geo-Accurate

      The same calculator loads different constants, safety factors, and units depending on your region — 145 lb/ft³ and ACI in the US, 2,400 kg/m³ and BS EN in the UK, 2,400 kg/m³ and AS 3600 in Australia. The banner always shows which standard is active.

    • Transparent

      Every result page shows the formula, the variables, and the constants used. Nothing is hidden behind "proprietary algorithm." If you think 6% waste is wrong for your job, the input is right there — change it and the result updates live.

    • Field-Tested

      We test against real deliveries, not textbook examples. When a slab calculator says 7.4 yd³ and the truck delivers 7.5 because the driver added a splash, that half-yard discrepancy is what we tune against. Field data beats theory every time.

    • Free Forever

      No subscription, no account, no export-to-PDF paywall. Every calculator, every formula, every print layout is available to anyone with a browser. Ads keep the lights on; they do not gate the tools.

    // HOW TO USE //

    Three Steps to a Defensible Estimate

    Every BuildMetric calculator follows the same three-step flow. Learn it once, and all 300 tools work the same way — no re-learning a new UI for every trade.

    1. 1

      Set Your Region and Units

      The banner at the top of every page shows your active standard, unit system, and currency. BuildMetric auto-detects on first visit — US gets Imperial with ACI 318-19, the UK gets Metric with BS EN 1992, Australia gets AS 3600, and so on. If the detection is wrong, one click fixes it, and your choice is remembered for 30 days. You can also flip units inside any calculator without changing your region.

    2. 2

      Enter Your Dimensions

      Each input has a stepper with a yellow − and + button, so you can tap to fine-tune without pulling up a keyboard on site. Hold the button for half a second and it accelerates. Type directly if you prefer — the result updates live as you type, debounced at 300ms. Every input shows its unit, and the unit converts instantly if you switch from Imperial to Metric mid-entry.

    3. 3

      Read the Result, Then Act On It

      The result panel shows the primary number in monospace — cubic yards, board feet, kWh, whatever the trade actually buys in — plus three or four secondary metrics that matter for the same job (waste-adjusted quantity, cost range, bag counts). The formula and its standard clause are printed below. From there you can save to your browser, download a PDF, copy a shareable link, or send the numbers to suppliers in your ZIP for a real quote.

    // STANDARDS //

    The Codes Behind the Numbers

    BuildMetric does not invent formulas. Every structural and material calculation traces back to a published standard, and the active standard is always shown in the result panel.

    • 🇺🇸 United States

      ACI 318-19

      Building Code Requirements for Structural Concrete. Governs reinforcement cover, development length, shear strength, and minimum slab thickness for US pours.

    • 🇬🇧 United Kingdom

      BS EN 1992

      Eurocode 2: Design of Concrete Structures, with the UK National Annex. Sets partial safety factors, concrete strength classes, and minimum reinforcement for UK projects.

    • 🇦🇺 Australia

      AS 3600

      Concrete Structures. Australia's primary concrete design standard, used for slab, footing, column, and reinforcement checks in AS-compliant builds.

    • 🇮🇳 India

      IS 456

      Plain and Reinforced Concrete — Code of Practice. Governs mix design, cover, development length, and reinforcement detailing for Indian construction.

    • 🇨🇦 Canada

      CSA A23.3

      Design of Concrete Structures. Canada's national concrete code, aligned with CSA A23.1 for materials and methods. Used for all Canadian calculations.

    • 🇪🇺 European Union

      Eurocode 2

      EN 1992-1-1 without a National Annex. Default baseline for EU countries where BuildMetric does not yet carry a country-specific annex.

    // FAQ //

    Questions Engineers Actually Ask

    Are BuildMetric calculators free to use?

    Yes. All 300 calculators on BuildMetric are free, with no signup, no subscription, and no paywall. We fund the platform through Google AdSense and optional supplier quote requests, which are never required to use a calculator. If you want the full legal version, it is spelled out in our Terms of Service — but the short answer is that the calculators stay free.

    Which building standards do the calculators follow?

    Calculators are aligned to the standard used in your region: ACI 318-19 in the United States, BS EN 1992 in the United Kingdom, AS 3600 in Australia, CSA A23.3 in Canada, IS 456 in India, and Eurocode 2 in the EU. The active standard is shown in the result panel of every calculator, along with the specific clause the formula follows. If you switch your region in the banner, the constants and safety factors update to match.

    Can I switch between imperial and metric units?

    Yes. Every calculator has an Imperial / Metric toggle in the header. BuildMetric auto-detects your region on first visit, then remembers your preference. You can change it at any time, and all inputs and results convert immediately. You can also override units per calculator without changing your saved region — useful if you are working on a project that specifies metric even though you are based in the US, or vice versa.

    How accurate are the material estimates?

    The formulas follow published standards and manufacturer data, and the outputs have been cross-checked against real pours, installs, and takeoffs — including 200+ concrete pours logged by three separate ready-mix suppliers in the US and UK. That said, estimates are intended for planning and procurement, not as a substitute for stamped engineering drawings or a licensed contractor's site assessment. A calculator cannot see your subgrade, your shade, or the truck that showed up with a different slump than ordered.

    Do you store the numbers I enter?

    No. Calculator inputs run entirely in your browser and are never sent to our servers. The Save Result button writes to your browser's localStorage, which stays on your device. The only data we receive is what you choose to submit through a supplier quote form or the newsletter signup, and only if you actually submit it. See our Privacy Policy for the full data flow.
