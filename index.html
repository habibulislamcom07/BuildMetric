    // WHY THIS EXISTS //

    Construction math fails on job sites. Not because it's hard — because it's slow.

    A foreman pouring a 24 × 36 ft slab doesn't need a CAD program. He needs cubic yards, bag count, and rebar weight in under thirty seconds — usually standing in the sun, gloves on, phone in one hand. Every extra tap is friction. Every spinner is a reason to guess instead of check.

    The tools that exist today fall into two categories, and both fail the same moment. Category one: thousand-page PDFs and ACI tables that are technically correct but useless in the field. Category two: web calculators that look clean until you enter a fractional dimension and the site reloads, flashes an ad, and asks you to sign up for a free trial of a $49/month takeoff platform. Neither is acceptable when the concrete truck is already on the way.

    BuildMetric was built against that specific failure. Every calculator on this site runs entirely in your browser, uses the same formulas a licensed engineer would use in a preliminary takeoff, and updates as you type. No submit button waiting for a server. No spinner. No signup wall. If your phone has the page loaded once, it will calculate on a site with zero bars.

    What "standard-based" actually means here

    A calculator is only as good as its formula. Our concrete tools reference ACI 318-19 for structural concrete and ACI 211.1 for mix proportioning. Structural lumber checks pull from NDS and span tables published by the American Wood Council. Electrical load calculations follow NEC Article 220, and HVAC sizing follows ACCA Manual J principles. For UK, EU, Australia, Canada, India, and the Gulf, the equivalent local codes — BS EN 1992, AS 3600, CSA A23.3, IS 456, and Eurocode 2 — are used where they diverge meaningfully from US practice. Every tool page lists the exact reference at the bottom, so you can verify the formula against your own codebook before you trust it.

    Where BuildMetric deliberately stops

    These are quantity calculators, not design tools. We compute how much concrete, how many studs, what wire gauge, how many solar panels. We do not stamp structural drawings, and we do not replace a licensed engineer's judgment on load paths, seismic detailing, or soil conditions. Any calculator that would require site-specific engineering — foundation design for expansive clay, for example — deliberately stops at the preliminary estimate and says so. That boundary is not a limitation. It is the reason the tools you can trust are trustworthy.

    Built for four people at once

    The same page has to work for a general contractor pricing a bid, a DIY homeowner pouring a patio, a project manager checking a subcontractor's numbers, and an engineer doing a fast sanity check. That is why every calculator defaults to sensible field values, exposes the waste factor as a toggle instead of hiding it, and lets you switch between imperial and metric without losing your inputs. The number matters. The unit is secondary.

    Three hundred calculators across twelve trades. All free. No account. That is the whole product.

    // HOW IT WORKS //

    Three steps. No tutorial required.

    The interaction pattern is identical across all 300 calculators. Learn it once on any tool and you have learned every tool on the site.

    1. STEP 01

      Enter your dimensions

      Tap the [ + ] and [ − ] steppers, or type directly into any field. Long-press a stepper to accelerate. Arrow keys work too. Smart step sizes mean a slab length moves in 0.5 ft increments near zero and 100 ft increments when you are pricing a parking lot.

    2. STEP 02

      Read the result instantly

      The result box updates as you type — no calculate button, no spinner, no page reload. Primary quantity is the big green number. Secondary metrics (bag count, waste, weight, cost) sit below it in mono type. Toggle the waste factor on or off with one tap.

    3. STEP 03

      Take the number to the field

      Copy the result, download a PDF for the permit file, or print the whole page for the truck. Every tool works offline once loaded — keep the tab open on site and it will keep calculating with zero bars. Nothing to install, nothing to log into.

    // COMMON QUESTIONS //

    Seven things people ask before they trust a free calculator

    Are BuildMetric calculators really free?

    Yes. Every calculator on BuildMetric is free to use with no signup, no paywall, and no subscription. The site is supported by display advertising and optional supplier lead referrals. There is no premium tier hiding behind a login, and no "unlock full results" gate on any tool. If you see a calculator on the site, every field and every result is available to you the same way it is to anyone else.

    Which building codes and standards do the calculators follow?

    Formulas reference ACI 318-19 and ACI 211.1 for concrete, AISC and NDS for steel and timber, ASHRAE and NEC for electrical and HVAC, and regional equivalents including BS EN 1992, AS 3600, CSA A23.3, and IS 456. Every tool page lists its specific reference at the bottom, including the clause or table number where applicable. If your local code differs from the default, you can switch the reference manually using the region selector on the tool page.

    Do the calculators work offline on a job site?

    After a page has loaded once, the calculation runs entirely in your browser with no network calls. You can keep a tool page open in a tab on-site and it will continue to work through intermittent signal loss. Nothing is saved to a server, nothing is uploaded, and the result updates the same way online or offline. If you close the tab, you will need to reload once on a connection before it works offline again.

    Can I switch between imperial and metric units?

    Yes. Every calculator has an Imperial/Metric toggle at the top of the input card. The site also auto-detects your region and sets the default system, but you can override it at any time and the choice is remembered across the site. When you switch units, your current inputs are converted automatically so you do not lose your place. Currency for cost estimators is detected the same way and can be changed from the same toggle.

    Are the results accurate enough for permit drawings?

    The calculators produce standard engineering quantities you would use in a takeoff, estimate, or preliminary design. Final permit documents and structural sign-offs should always be verified by a licensed engineer or architect in your jurisdiction. Any calculator that would require site-specific engineering — foundation design for expansive clay, for example — deliberately stops at the preliminary estimate and says so on the page rather than producing a number that looks more authoritative than it is.

    Do I need an account, and is my data saved?

    No account is required, and no calculator input is sent to a server. Everything you type stays in your browser. The only optional exception is a "Save Result" button on tool pages, which stores the result in your browser's local storage so you can find it later — this never leaves your device and you can clear it any time by clearing browser data. The B2B supplier form is the one place where you actively submit contact details, and that only happens if you choose to fill it in.

    How does BuildMetric make money if everything is free?

    Two ways, both visible on the site. First, standard display advertising shown in clearly labelled slots — never between calculator inputs, never as a popup, and never covering the result you came for. Second, optional supplier referral: if you fill the "Get supplier quotes" form, participating suppliers pay a small fee for the introduction. Neither requires a subscription, and neither affects which calculators we build or what the results show.
