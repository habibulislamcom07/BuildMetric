Why we built this

A calculator is only useful if you can trust the number

Anyone who has ordered materials for a project knows the moment of doubt. You have a figure on your phone, you are standing in a supplier's yard, and you have to decide whether to order what the calculator says or add "a bit extra" because you are not sure the tool was right. That uncertainty is expensive. Under-order and you lose a day waiting for a delivery. Over-order and you pay for material you will never use, then pay again to have it taken away.

BuildMetric exists to remove that doubt. We are not a directory, a lead broker or a marketplace. We are a calculation platform. Every tool on this site is built around a formula we can point to, with default values we can justify, and a waste factor you control rather than one we hide. When a calculator tells you that you need 4.8 cubic yards of concrete, we show you the arithmetic that produced that figure so you can check it against your own drawings.

The site is built for two markets deliberately. The United States works in feet, inches and cubic yards, and buys concrete in 60 lb or 80 lb bags or by the truckload. The United Kingdom works in metres, centimetres and cubic metres, and buys materials in different pack sizes from different merchants. A calculator that only speaks one of those languages is half-useless to the other market. Every BuildMetric tool ships with a one-click unit toggle, and the underlying maths converts precisely rather than approximately.

We also built for a specific kind of user. Not the person who wants an article about concrete — the person who wants a number, right now, on a phone, with one hand, standing in a garden. That is why the calculator sits at the top of every page and the explanatory content sits underneath it. Read as much or as little as you need.

Methodology

How BuildMetric calculators actually work

Every calculator follows the same four-step structure. Understanding it helps you sanity-check any result.

1. Geometry first

The first step is always pure geometry: length × width × depth for a slab, surface area for paint or tile, linear run for fencing. This part must be exactly right, because every later step multiplies whatever error exists here. A 5% error in your measured length becomes a 5% error in your material order, which on a large slab can be several hundred dollars.

2. Convert to a single working unit

Mixed units are where most manual calculations go wrong. Thickness is usually given in inches while length and width are in feet; concrete is sold in cubic yards while bags are rated in cubic feet. Our calculators convert everything to a single internal unit before doing any arithmetic, which eliminates the classic "divided by 12 in the wrong place" mistake.

3. Apply the material yield

Once we have a volume or an area, we apply the published yield for the material. For concrete, one 80 lb bag of premixed concrete yields approximately 0.6 cubic feet, and one 60 lb bag yields approximately 0.45 cubic feet. For paint, coverage is typically 350 square feet per gallon per coat on a prepared surface. For drywall, a standard sheet is 4 ft × 8 ft, or 32 square feet. These yields come from manufacturer technical data sheets and match the figures professional estimators use.

4. Add your waste factor

Finally, the waste factor is applied. Waste is not padding — it is a real, measurable quantity. Cut-offs on a diagonal tile layout, breakage during delivery, a form that bows slightly under wet concrete, a board that splits on the last screw. Professional estimators apply waste factors as a matter of routine. Ours are editable because the correct figure depends entirely on your layout, and only you know what that looks like.

Accuracy policy

Five rules every BuildMetric calculator follows

  1. The formula is published. Every calculator page shows the exact arithmetic being used. If you cannot verify it, you should not trust it.
  2. Defaults are sourced, not invented. Material yields come from manufacturer data sheets and published estimating standards. Where a range exists, we use a mid-range value and tell you the range.
  3. The waste factor is editable and visible. We never silently add a percentage to a result. You see the waste factor, you control it, and the raw figure is always shown alongside the adjusted one.
  4. Units convert exactly. We use precise conversion constants (1 cubic yard = 0.764555 cubic metres, not a rounded 0.76) so results stay consistent when you switch between systems.
  5. Results are planning estimates. No calculator can account for site conditions, code requirements, or structural design. We say this on every page rather than burying it.

Every calculator is reviewed against its source formula before publication and re-checked when material standards or typical pricing change. If you believe a result is wrong, we want to know — write to contact@buildmetric.org with the inputs you used and we will investigate and publish a correction if one is needed.

US vs UK

The measurement differences that actually trip people up

If you are used to one system and working on a project in the other, the conversions are the easy part. The traps are in the conventions.

  • Concrete is sold differently. In the US, ready-mix is ordered in cubic yards and bags are sold by weight (60 lb or 80 lb). In the UK, ready-mix is ordered in cubic metres and bags are typically 25 kg. A "bag" means something completely different depending on which side of the Atlantic you are on.
  • Lumber and timber are nominal. A US "2×4" is actually 1½" × 3½". UK timber is usually sold at its actual finished size. Never assume a nominal dimension is the real one.
  • Plywood and sheet goods differ in size. The US standard sheet is 4 ft × 8 ft (32 sq ft). UK sheet materials are commonly 2400 mm × 1200 mm (2.88 m²), which is very slightly larger.
  • Paint coverage is quoted differently. US products quote square feet per gallon; UK products quote square metres per litre. The underlying coverage is similar but the numbers look nothing alike.
  • Thickness conventions differ. US flatwork thickness is quoted in inches; UK flatwork is quoted in millimetres. A "4 inch" slab and a "100 mm" slab are within 2 mm of each other, but the numbers never appear the same.

BuildMetric handles all of this through the unit toggle, but we also recommend checking the label on the actual product you are buying before you order, because pack sizes change and merchants sometimes stock imported formats.

How to choose your waste factor

The waste factor is the single most argued-about input in construction estimating. Too low and you run short mid-job. Too high and you pay for material you throw away. A practical guide:

  • 5% — simple rectangular layouts with few cuts, such as a square patio or a basic shed base.
  • 10% — the safe default for most flatwork, framing and general material orders.
  • 15% — diagonal tile layouts, complex room shapes, roofs with multiple hips and valleys.
  • 20%+ — intricate patterns, heritage restoration, or any job where a second delivery would cost more than the surplus material.

One practical rule: if the material is cheap and a return trip is expensive, err high. If the material is expensive and returns are easy, err low.

Process

A six-step workflow that saves money on every project

  1. Measure twice, in the same units. Write down every dimension in one unit system before you touch a calculator.
  2. Run the raw calculation first with zero waste. This tells you the theoretical minimum and helps you spot input errors.
  3. Add your waste factor and note it down. Write the percentage next to the figure so you remember what you did.
  4. Compare supply options. Bagged versus ready-mix, sheet versus bulk — the cheapest unit price is not always the cheapest total once delivery and minimum orders are counted.
  5. Save or copy the result before you leave the house. Use the Save button for your own records or Copy to paste into a message to your supplier.
  6. Confirm with the supplier before ordering. Pack sizes change, stock varies, and a two-minute phone call beats a wasted trip.

Honest limits

When a calculator is not enough

We would rather lose a user's trust by being honest than gain it by overpromising. BuildMetric calculators are planning tools. They are not a substitute for any of the following:

  • Structural design. Load-bearing beams, headers and foundations need engineering input based on span tables, species, grade and local code. Our deck calculator will tell you this directly.
  • Permits and code compliance. Requirements vary by state, county, borough and even street. Check with your local authority before you build.
  • Site conditions. Soil type, drainage, slope, frost depth and access all change both material quantities and methods. A calculator cannot see your site.
  • Safety-critical work. Gas, electrical, structural alterations and anything involving asbestos should go to a qualified professional.
  • Fixed-price quotes. A contractor's quote includes labour, overhead, contingency and risk that no material calculator can model.

Use BuildMetric to plan, to budget, and to sanity-check. Then confirm the details with a qualified professional before committing money.

Reference

Construction terms used on this site

  • Cubic yard (cu yd) — 27 cubic feet. The standard US unit for ordering ready-mix concrete.
  • Cubic metre (m³) — 1,000 litres. The standard UK and European unit for ready-mix concrete.
  • Square (roofing) — 100 square feet of roof surface. Shingles are sold by the bundle, with roughly three bundles to a square.
  • Pitch multiplier — the factor by which a roof's footprint area must be multiplied to get its true surface area, based on slope.
  • Waste factor — a percentage added to a raw material quantity to cover cuts, breakage and measurement error.
  • Coverage rate — the area a given quantity of material will cover, such as square feet per gallon of paint.
  • Nominal size — the name a material is sold under, which may differ from its actual finished dimensions.
  • Footing — the below-ground concrete base that spreads a structure's load onto the soil.

Frequently asked questions

Is BuildMetric free to use?

Yes. Every BuildMetric calculator, guide and quick estimate tool is free to use. There is no paywall, no trial period and no signup requirement to get a result. You can use any calculator as many times as you need, on any device, without creating an account.

Do I need an account to save my results?

No. The Save button stores your inputs and results privately in your own browser using local storage. Nothing is uploaded to BuildMetric servers, nothing is shared with third parties, and nothing leaves your device. If you clear your browser data, saved calculations are cleared with it — that is the trade-off we accept in exchange for not holding your personal data.

How accurate are BuildMetric calculators?

Our calculators use published industry formulas and standard material yields, such as 0.6 cubic feet per 80 lb bag of concrete mix or 27 cubic feet per cubic yard. Every formula is documented on the calculator page so you can verify it yourself. Results are planning estimates, not a substitute for a site-specific quote, and we say so on every page rather than burying it in small print.

Do you support both US and UK measurement units?

Yes. Every calculator has a one-click Imperial and Metric toggle. Imperial mode works in feet, inches and cubic yards for the US market. Metric mode works in metres, centimetres and cubic metres for the UK and European market. The underlying conversion uses precise constants rather than rounded approximations, so switching between systems does not introduce drift into your results.

How does the waste factor work?

The waste factor adds a percentage to your raw material quantity to cover offcuts, breakage and measurement error. We default to 10 percent for most flatwork and 5 to 7 percent for simple rectangular layouts, but the field is fully editable because every site is different. A diagonal tile layout needs more waste than a straight grid; a roof with multiple hips and valleys needs more than a simple gable. You know your site — the calculator should not hide that decision from you.

Can I save, share or copy my calculation?

Yes. Every result card includes Save, Share and Copy buttons. Save keeps the calculation on your device, Share sends a link or text summary through your phone or email using the native share sheet, and Copy places a formatted result summary on your clipboard ready to paste into an email, quote or message. All three functions work without a login, and none of them transmit your data to our servers.

How does BuildMetric make money, and does it affect the results?

BuildMetric is supported by display advertising. Advertising never influences a formula, a default value or a recommendation. Our accuracy policy is published on this page and every calculator is reviewed against published industry standards before it goes live. If we ever add sponsored placements or affiliate links in the future, they will be clearly labelled and they will never change the number a calculator returns.
