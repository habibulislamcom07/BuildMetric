What Is a Paint Calculator?

A paint calculator is a tool that estimates how much paint you need to cover a given area — measured in litres, gallons or the number of paint cans required. Instead of guessing and ending up with too little paint (which means a trip back to the store and a visible mismatch in colour or finish) or too much (which means wasted money sitting in your garage), you enter your room dimensions and the calculator works out the exact quantity.

Paint quantity depends on four main factors: the total surface area to be painted, the number of coats you plan to apply, the coverage rate of the paint you are using, and any surfaces that do not need painting (such as doors, windows and built-in furniture).

How to Use This Paint Calculator

  1. Enter the room dimensions. Provide the length, width and wall height of the room in your chosen unit system.
  2. Select doors and windows. Use the dropdowns to indicate how many doors and windows are on the walls you are painting.
  3. Choose the number of coats. Two coats are standard for most interior walls.
  4. Select the paint type. Interior and exterior paint have slightly different coverage rates.
  5. Click Calculate. The result shows the total paintable area, paint per coat, total paint required and recommended can sizes.

Formula Used by This Calculator

All calculations are performed internally in metric units, then converted back to your preferred unit system for display.

Wall Area

Wall Area = 2 × (Length + Width) × Height This gives the total wall surface area of a rectangular room, in square units.

Paintable Area

Paintable Area = Wall Area − (Doors × Door Area) − (Windows × Window Area) Standard door area: 1.8 m² (about 20 sq ft)
Standard window area: 1.5 m² (about 16 sq ft)

Paint Quantity

Paint per Coat = Paintable Area ÷ Coverage Rate Interior coverage: ~10 m² per litre (~350 sq ft per gallon)
Exterior coverage: ~8 m² per litre (~300 sq ft per gallon)
Total Paint = Paint per Coat × Number of Coats

Worked Example

Suppose you are painting a room that is 10 feet long, 12 feet wide and 8 feet high, with 1 door and 2 windows. You plan to apply 2 coats of interior paint.

  1. Convert to metres: 10 ft = 3.05 m, 12 ft = 3.66 m, 8 ft = 2.44 m
  2. Calculate wall area: 2 × (3.05 + 3.66) × 2.44 = 32.74 m²
  3. Subtract doors and windows: 32.74 − (1 × 1.8) − (2 × 1.5) = 27.94 m²
  4. Paint per coat (interior): 27.94 ÷ 10 = 2.79 litres
  5. Total paint (2 coats): 2.79 × 2 = 5.59 litres

So for this room, you would buy approximately 5.6 litres of paint — or about 1.5 gallons in imperial units. Since paint is usually sold in 1-litre, 4-litre or 5-litre cans (or 1-gallon cans), you would buy one 5-litre can and one 1-litre can to be safe.

Pro Tip: Always buy slightly more paint than the calculator suggests. Paint colours can vary slightly between batches, so having a small reserve for touch-ups is essential.

Frequently Asked Questions

A 10×12 room with 8-foot ceilings and 2 coats typically needs about 5.5 to 6 litres (1.5 gallons) of paint, assuming one door and two windows. The exact amount depends on your wall height, the number of doors and windows, the paint's coverage rate and whether you are painting the ceiling or trim as well. Use the calculator above for your exact room dimensions.

Two coats are standard for most interior walls. One coat rarely provides full coverage or an even finish, even with premium paint. Three coats may be needed when painting with dark colours over light walls, when covering strong colours, or when using lower-quality paint. Primer may be required for bare drywall, patched surfaces or dramatic colour changes.

Most interior wall paints cover approximately 10 to 12 square metres per litre (about 350 to 400 square feet per gallon) in a single coat. Exterior paints typically cover less — around 8 square metres per litre (about 300 square feet per gallon) — because exterior surfaces are more porous. Coverage rates are printed on the paint can and should always be checked before buying.
