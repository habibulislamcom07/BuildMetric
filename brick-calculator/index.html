What Is a Brick Calculator?

A brick calculator is a tool that estimates how many bricks, how much mortar and how much sand you need to build a brick wall. Instead of guessing and ending up with too few bricks (which means a trip back to the supplier and the risk of a colour-batch mismatch) or too many (which means wasted money sitting in your yard), you enter your wall dimensions and brick size, and the calculator works out the exact quantity.

Brick quantity depends on several factors: the total wall surface area, the size of the brick you are using, the thickness of the mortar joint (typically 10 mm or 3/8 inch), the wall thickness (single skin or double skin / cavity wall), the number of openings such as doors and windows, and the waste allowance for cuts and breakages.

Bricks are one of the oldest and most durable building materials in the world. They are used in residential, commercial and industrial construction for walls, foundations, fireplaces, chimneys, retaining walls and decorative features. Whether you are building a garden wall, an extension, or an entire house, accurate brick estimation saves time, money and frustration.

Whether you are building a wall in Texas with US modular bricks, a house extension in London with UK standard bricks, a compound wall in India with Indian standard bricks, or a garden fence in Australia with Australian standard bricks, this calculator gives you an accurate estimate in both imperial and metric units.

How to Use This Brick Calculator

Using this brick calculator is straightforward. Follow these six steps:

  1. Enter the wall dimensions. Measure the length and height of the wall you are building. For an irregularly shaped wall, divide it into rectangles, calculate each area separately, and add the results together. Provide the dimensions in your chosen unit system — feet/inches or metres/millimetres.
  2. Select the brick size. Different regions use different standard bricks. US modular, US queen, UK standard, Indian standard and Australian standard bricks all have different dimensions. Choose the one that matches your supply.
  3. Choose the wall thickness. A single-skin wall uses one brick width. A double-skin (cavity) wall uses two bricks with a gap between them. Double-skin walls need roughly twice as many bricks.
  4. Select doors and windows. Use the dropdowns to indicate how many doors and windows are in the wall. The calculator subtracts their typical area automatically — a standard door is about 20 sq ft (1.8 m²) and a standard window is about 16 sq ft (1.5 m²).
  5. Set the waste allowance. Use 5% for simple walls, 10% for standard walls (recommended), and 15% for walls with many corners, openings or decorative patterns.
  6. Click Calculate. The calculator returns the total wall area, number of bricks needed, mortar required (kg / bags), and sand required (cubic feet / cubic metres).

Pro Tip: Always order 5–10% extra bricks beyond the calculated quantity. Bricks can be damaged during transport or installation, and having spares on hand means you can replace a cracked brick years later without a visible mismatch.

Formula Used by This Calculator

All calculations are performed internally in metric units, then converted back to your preferred unit system for display. The following formulas are used:

Step 1 — Wall Area

Wall Area = Wall Length × Wall Height For irregular walls, divide into rectangles and sum the areas.

Step 2 — Subtract Doors and Windows

Net Area = Wall Area − (Doors × 20) − (Windows × 16) Standard door: 20 sq ft (1.8 m²). Standard window: 16 sq ft (1.5 m²).

Step 3 — Bricks per Unit Area

Bricks per sq ft = 1 ÷ ((Brick Length + Joint) × (Brick Height + Joint)) Standard mortar joint: 3/8 inch (10 mm).
US modular brick: about 6.9 bricks per sq ft (single skin).
UK standard brick: about 60 bricks per m² (single skin).

Step 4 — Total Bricks (Single Skin)

Total Bricks = Net Area × Bricks per Unit Area For double-skin walls, multiply by 2.

Step 5 — Apply Waste Allowance

Total Bricks with Waste = Total Bricks × (1 + Waste% ÷ 100) Standard waste: 5% for simple walls, up to 15% for complex walls.

Step 6 — Mortar Required

Mortar (kg) = Total Bricks × 0.3 kg per brick About 0.3 kg of dry mortar mix per standard brick.
Mortar bags: 1 × 60 lb bag = 27 kg (US), 1 × 25 kg bag = 25 kg (metric).

Step 7 — Sand Required

Sand (kg) = Mortar (kg) × 0.75 Mortar mix is typically 1 part cement to 4–6 parts sand.
Sand volume: 1,600 kg = 1 cubic metre.

Understanding the Variables

  • Wall Area: Total surface area of the wall, excluding openings.
  • Brick Size: Actual dimensions of the brick, in mm or inches.
  • Mortar Joint: The gap between bricks, typically 10 mm (3/8 inch).
  • Wall Thickness: Single skin (1 brick) or double skin (2 bricks).
  • Waste %: Extra bricks for cuts, breakages and future repairs.
  • Mortar per Brick: Approximately 0.3 kg of dry mortar mix per standard brick.

Why Round Up, Not Down?

Brick calculations involve rounding in two places: bricks and mortar bags. Always round up, never down. If your calculation gives 1,247 bricks, order 1,250. If it gives 14.2 bags of mortar, order 15. Running short mid-project is far more expensive than having spares at the end — especially if the supplier's next batch has a slightly different colour.

Standard Brick Sizes Around the World

  • US Modular: 7-5/8 × 3-5/8 × 2-1/4 inches (194 × 92 × 57 mm)
  • US Queen: 7-5/8 × 2-3/4 × 2-3/4 inches (194 × 70 × 70 mm)
  • UK Standard: 215 × 102.5 × 65 mm (8.5 × 4 × 2.6 inches)
  • India Standard: 190 × 90 × 90 mm (7.5 × 3.5 × 3.5 inches)
  • Australia Standard: 230 × 110 × 76 mm (9 × 4.3 × 3 inches)

Always verify the actual size of your bricks before ordering — nominal sizes can differ from actual sizes by a few millimetres, which adds up over a large wall.

Worked Example

Let us work through three examples — a simple garden wall, a double-skin house extension, and a wall with multiple openings.

Example 1 — Simple Garden Wall (10 ft × 6 ft, single skin, US Modular, 5% waste)

  1. Wall area: 10 × 6 = 60 sq ft
  2. No openings: Net area = 60 sq ft
  3. Bricks per sq ft (US Modular, single skin): 6.9 bricks
  4. Total bricks: 60 × 6.9 = 414 bricks
  5. Waste (5%): 414 × 0.05 = 20.7 bricks
  6. Total bricks to order: 414 + 21 = 435 bricks
  7. Mortar required: 435 × 0.3 = 130.5 kg (about 5 bags of 27 kg)

Result: You need 435 bricks and about 5 bags of mortar mix (27 kg each).

Example 2 — Double-Skin House Extension (5 m × 2.7 m, double skin, UK Standard, 10% waste)

  1. Wall area: 5 × 2.7 = 13.5 m²
  2. No openings: Net area = 13.5 m²
  3. Bricks per m² (UK Standard, single skin): 60 bricks
  4. Single-skin bricks: 13.5 × 60 = 810 bricks
  5. Double-skin: 810 × 2 = 1,620 bricks
  6. Waste (10%): 1,620 × 0.10 = 162 bricks
  7. Total bricks to order: 1,620 + 162 = 1,782 bricks
  8. Mortar required: 1,782 × 0.3 = 534.6 kg (about 22 bags of 25 kg)

Result: You need 1,782 bricks and about 22 bags of mortar mix (25 kg each).

Example 3 — Wall with Openings (12 ft × 9 ft, single skin, US Modular, 1 door, 2 windows, 10% waste)

  1. Wall area: 12 × 9 = 108 sq ft
  2. Subtract door: 108 − 20 = 88 sq ft
  3. Subtract windows: 88 − (2 × 16) = 56 sq ft
  4. Net area: 56 sq ft
  5. Bricks per sq ft (US Modular, single skin): 6.9 bricks
  6. Total bricks: 56 × 6.9 = 386 bricks
  7. Waste (10%): 386 × 0.10 = 38.6 bricks
  8. Total bricks to order: 386 + 39 = 425 bricks
  9. Mortar required: 425 × 0.3 = 127.5 kg (about 5 bags of 27 kg)

Result: You need 425 bricks and about 5 bags of mortar mix.

Pro Tip: For walls with decorative patterns (herringbone, basketweave, Flemish bond), add an extra 5% to your waste allowance. These patterns require more cuts and produce more breakages — and running out mid-project is far more expensive than having a few spare bricks.

Types of Bricks

Not all bricks are the same. Different types are made for different applications:

  • Common Burnt Clay Bricks: General-purpose construction. Suitable for interior and exterior walls, but not for exposed applications where appearance matters.
  • Face Bricks: Higher quality, uniform colour and texture. Used for exposed exterior walls where appearance matters.
  • Engineering Bricks: Strong, dense, low water absorption. Used for foundations, sewers, retaining walls and engineering applications.
  • Concrete Bricks: Made from cement and aggregate. Cheaper than clay, but less durable in freeze-thaw climates.
  • Fly Ash Bricks: Made from fly ash, cement and sand. Lighter and more eco-friendly than clay bricks. Popular in India and Asia.
  • Fire Bricks (Refractory): Heat-resistant. Used for fireplaces, pizza ovens, kilns and industrial furnaces.
  • Calcium Silicate Bricks: Made from sand and lime. Uniform shape, good thermal insulation. Common in Europe.
  • Hollow Bricks: Have holes or cavities. Lighter, better insulation, require less mortar. Used for non-load-bearing walls.

Industry Standards and Codes

Brick construction must comply with several industry standards and building codes:

  • ASTM C216: Standard Specification for Facing Brick (USA)
  • ASTM C652: Standard Specification for Hollow Brick
  • ASTM C67: Standard Test Methods for Sampling and Testing Brick
  • BS EN 771-1: Specification for Clay Masonry Units (UK / Europe)
  • BS 5628: Code of Practice for Use of Masonry (UK)
  • IS 1077: Common Burnt Clay Building Bricks Specification (India)
  • AS/NZS 4455: Masonry Units and Segment Pavers (Australia / New Zealand)
  • IBC (International Building Code): Structural masonry requirements
  • TMS 402: Building Code Requirements for Masonry Structures

Always check your local building codes before starting any brick wall project. Requirements vary significantly by region, especially for seismic zones, frost depth, fire resistance and thermal performance.

  • Face Bricks: $0.50–$1.20 per brick

Tools and Equipment Needed

To build a brick wall, you will need the following tools:

  • Essential: Brick trowel, spirit level, masonry line, club hammer, cold chisel, brick jointer, pointing trowel, tape measure, mixing board or wheelbarrow
  • Recommended: Brick tongs, bolster chisel, plugging chisel, corner blocks, mortar hoe, hosepipe, pointing trowel
  • Safety: Safety glasses, dust mask, gloves, steel-toe boots, knee pads, sun protection
  • Material Preparation: Mortar mixer (or drill with mixing paddle), mortar board, buckets

Safety first: brick cutting creates silica dust, which is harmful to lungs. Always wear a dust mask when cutting bricks, and use a wet saw or dust suppression system when possible.

Maintenance and Care

Proper maintenance extends the life of your brick wall:

  • Inspect annually: Check for cracked bricks, crumbling mortar, efflorescence and water damage.
  • Repoint mortar: Replace crumbling mortar joints every 25–30 years. Match the original mortar composition.
  • Clean efflorescence: White salt deposits (efflorescence) indicate moisture. Brush off with a stiff brush and address the water source.
  • Seal properly: Use a breathable masonry sealer, never a non-breathable one. Bricks need to "breathe".
  • Fix cracks promptly: Small cracks allow water infiltration. Fill them with mortar or sealant.
  • Keep gutters clean: Water overflow damages brick walls. Clean gutters regularly.
  • Trim vegetation: Keep vines and plants away from brick walls. Roots can damage mortar joints.
  • Avoid pressure washing: High-pressure water damages brick surfaces. Use low-pressure washing or gentle cleaning.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Even experienced bricklayers make mistakes when calculating brick quantities. Here are the most common ones:

  • Forgetting mortar joint thickness: Ignoring the 10 mm joint overestimates brick count by 5–8%.
  • Using the wrong brick size: US Modular, UK Standard and Indian Standard bricks have very different dimensions. Check the actual size of your supply.
  • Forgetting the waste allowance: Always add 5–15% depending on wall complexity and number of openings.
  • Not subtracting openings: Doors and windows do not need bricks. Subtract them for an accurate estimate.
  • Miscalculating double-skin walls: Double-skin walls need roughly twice the bricks of single-skin walls.
  • Underestimating mortar: Mortar is used for both bedding and pointing. Budget 0.3 kg per brick.
  • Forgetting sand: Mortar requires both cement and sand. Plan on about 0.75 kg of sand per kg of mortar.
  • Rounding down: Always round up — never down — when ordering bricks and mortar.
  • Mixing metric and imperial: Keep all measurements in the same unit system throughout. Our calculator handles conversions automatically.
  • Ignoring weather: Extreme heat or cold affects mortar curing. Adjust mix and cure time based on weather.
