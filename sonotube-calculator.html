Quick Answer: How Many Bags of Concrete for a Sonotube?

The number of bags needed depends on the tube's diameter and depth. As a general rule of thumb using 80lb bags: an 8-inch tube requires about 2 bags per 4 feet of depth, a 10-inch tube requires 3 bags, and a 12-inch tube requires 4 bags. Our calculator above provides the exact bag count based on your specific pier dimensions and regional bag sizes (US, UK, or AU).

The Engineering Behind Concrete Piers and Sonotubes

In residential construction, the structural integrity of a deck, carport, or fence relies entirely on what is hidden underground. Sonotubes (fiber forms used to cast circular concrete columns) are the industry standard in North America for creating deep, frost-resistant footings. They prevent the surrounding soil from caving in during the pour, ensuring the concrete cures with a smooth, uniform diameter that maximizes load-bearing capacity.

However, simply digging a hole and pouring concrete is a recipe for structural failure. You must calculate the exact volume required to fill the tube to the correct height. If the concrete falls short, you create a "cold joint"—a weak seam where old and new concrete meet—which severely compromises the pier's strength. Over-ordering, on the other hand, wastes money and leaves you with heavy, unusable bags of hardened concrete.

Our Sonotube Calculator solves this by using the standard engineering formula for the volume of a cylinder: Volume = π × r² × h. We automatically convert your measurements into the exact number of 40lb, 60lb, or 80lb bags required, accounting for the standard yield of each mix.

The Math: Calculating Concrete Volume for Round Piers

To accurately estimate how much concrete you need for a circular footing, you must treat the hole as a perfect cylinder.

Step 1: Find the Radius (r)

The radius is half of the diameter. If you are using a 12-inch Sonotube, your radius is 6 inches.

Step 2: Calculate the Area (πr²)

Square the radius and multiply it by Pi (3.14159).
For a 12-inch tube: 6 × 6 × 3.14159 = 113.1 square inches.

Step 3: Calculate the Volume (Area × Height)

Multiply the area by the depth of your hole. If your hole is 48 inches deep:
113.1 sq in × 48 in = 5,428.8 cubic inches.

Because concrete bags are measured in cubic feet, you must convert cubic inches to cubic feet by dividing by 1,728.
5,428.8 ÷ 1,728 = 3.14 cubic feet.

Since a standard 80lb bag of concrete yields approximately 0.60 cubic feet, you would need roughly 5.2 bags (so you buy 6 bags to be safe).

Instead of doing this complex math manually, simply input your dimensions into our tool above. Our calculator handles the conversions instantly, ensuring you purchase the exact number of bags required for your project, whether you are using US Imperial bag sizes or Metric weights.

Installation Best Practices: Beyond the Volume Math

Calculating the exact volume of concrete is only the first step. If the Sonotube is not installed correctly, the structural integrity of the entire pier is compromised. Proper installation ensures the load transfers safely from the structure above to the stable soil below.

1. Digging to the Correct Frost Depth

In the US (per IRC R403.1) and the UK (per BS 8500), footings must extend below the local frost line. If the soil freezes and expands (frost heave) beneath your pier, it can lift the entire structure, causing catastrophic damage to decks and foundations. For example, in northern US states like Minnesota, the frost depth can be 60 inches or more. In the UK, a minimum depth of 450mm to 1m is typical, depending on the region. Australia's NCC guidelines vary by state, as frost heave is rare, but reactive clay soils (AS 2870) dictate deeper piers to reach stable strata.

2. Belling the Base (Spread Footings)

To increase the load-bearing capacity of a pier without pouring a massive concrete cylinder all the way to the surface, contractors often "bell" the bottom of the footing. This means flaring the hole out at the base to create a wider pad. When calculating your concrete volume, remember that a belled footing requires significantly more concrete. If you are belling your piers, you must calculate the volume of the cylinder and add the volume of the bell (truncated cone) to get the total concrete required.

3. Bracing and Backfilling

The hydrostatic pressure of wet concrete is immense. When pouring an 8-foot tall Sonotube, the weight of the concrete can cause the bottom of the tube to blow out if not properly backfilled. Backfill the hole around the tube with soil in 6-inch lifts, tamping it down firmly as you go. Above grade, you must brace the Sonotube with stakes and lumber to keep it perfectly plumb during the pour. Never rely on the tube to hold itself straight.

4. Managing Water in the Hole

If your hole fills with groundwater, do not pour concrete directly into the water. Excess water dilutes the cement paste, resulting in weak, crumbly concrete (often called "honeycombing"). Pump the water out immediately before pouring. If the water table is too high, consider using a Quick-Set concrete mix or pouring the concrete slightly "wet" and letting it displace the water, though pumping is always the preferred engineering approach.

Costing: Bags vs. Ready-Mix Concrete

Once you know your total concrete volume, you have to decide between buying bags (50lb, 60lb, 80lb) or ordering ready-mix concrete by the truckload.

The 10-Bag Rule: A standard rule of thumb in the construction industry is that if your project requires more than 10 to 15 bags of concrete, it is more economical and physically easier to order ready-mix concrete. Mixing 20 bags of concrete by hand in a wheelbarrow is exhausting and time-consuming, and the resulting mix may lack the consistency of a machine-mixed batch.

However, for a small fence or a deck with just 3 or 4 piers, bags are the way to go. Our calculator above instantly provides both the total volume (so you can call a ready-mix supplier) and the number of bags (so you can estimate the cost of going the DIY route). Always check the yield of the bags you are buying; a 60lb bag yields less than an 80lb bag, so the total count will change.

Frequently Asked Questions

Sonotube & Pier FAQs

Direct answers to common questions about pouring concrete footings and piers.

01

How many bags of concrete do I need for a 12-inch Sonotube?
For a standard 12-inch diameter Sonotube, you will need approximately 4 bags of 80lb concrete for every 4 feet of depth. If you are using 60lb bags, you will need about 5.5 bags. To get the exact number based on your specific hole depth, input your dimensions into the calculator above.
02

Do I need to put rebar inside a Sonotube?
Yes. While a pier can support compressive loads without rebar, adding a vertical piece of rebar (usually #4 or #5) is highly recommended to handle lateral loads (like wind or soil shifting) and to tie the pier to the structure above. Use our Rebar Calculator to determine the exact length and quantity needed for your piers.
03

How deep should I dig my concrete piers?
The depth of your pier is dictated by the local frost line and soil conditions. In the US (IRC code), footings must extend below the frost line (which can range from 12 inches in the south to 60+ inches in the north). In the UK, typical depths are 450mm to 1m. Always check your local building regulations before digging.
04

What happens if my hole fills with water before pouring?
You should always pump or bail the water out before pouring concrete. Pouring concrete directly into standing water dilutes the cement paste, severely weakening the final concrete mix and leading to crumbling (honeycombing). If the water table is too high, consider using a rapid-set mix or consult a structural engineer for alternative foundation options.
05

How long does concrete take to cure in a Sonotube?
Concrete sets in about 24 to 48 hours, but it reaches its full structural strength (cures) over 28 days. You can usually remove the cardboard Sonotube after 24-48 hours, but you should wait at least 7 days before placing heavy structural loads (like a deck frame) on the piers.
06

Should I use bagged concrete or ready-mix for my piers?
As a general rule, if your total project requires more than 10 to 15 bags of concrete, it is more cost-effective and physically less demanding to order ready-mix concrete. Mixing dozens of bags by hand is exhausting and can lead to inconsistent batches. Our calculator gives you the total volume in cubic feet so you can easily order from a supplier.
07

How do I keep the Sonotube straight and stable?
Backfilling the hole with soil in 6-inch increments and tamping it firmly is the best way to keep the underground portion straight. For the above-grade portion, you must brace the tube using wooden stakes and screws to prevent it from bowing or blowing out under the hydrostatic pressure of the wet concrete.
