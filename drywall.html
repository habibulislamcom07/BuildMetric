The Ultimate Guide to Drywall Estimation

Drywall (plasterboard) installation is one of the most visible parts of any renovation. A poorly estimated job leads to visible seams, cracked joints, and wasted material. Our calculators help you determine the exact number of 4x8 or 4x12 sheets, the gallons of joint compound, and the linear feet of tape. Pro Tip: Always add 10% waste for cuts, and use 5/8" thick drywall on ceilings for sag resistance.

The Anatomy of Drywall Installation

Drywall is a simple material—a gypsum core sandwiched between layers of heavy paper—but its installation is a multi-step process that requires precision at every stage.

1. Hanging the Sheets

The goal is to minimize the number of joints (seams). Long sheets (like 12-foot) reduce butt joints, creating a smoother finish, but they are heavier and harder to maneuver. Always hang the ceiling first, then the walls, to provide a "lift" for the top edge of the wall boards.

Always use the correct screws: coarse-thread for wood studs, fine-thread for metal studs. Screws should be driven just below the surface of the paper, without breaking it. Use our Drywall Calculator to find exact sheet and screw counts.

2. Taping and Mudding (The 3-Coat System)

This is where the art comes in. Joint compound (mud) and paper tape are used to hide the seams. The professional standard is a 3-coat system:

  • First Coat (Tape Coat): Embed the paper tape into a bed of mud. This is the structural bond.
  • Second Coat (Fill Coat): A wider layer of mud is applied over the tape to fill in the recess (the tapered edge of the sheet).
  • Third Coat (Finish Coat): A very thin, wide layer of mud is applied to feather the edges. This requires a 10-inch or 12-inch taping knife.

Our Drywall Finishing Calculator gives you the exact gallons of mud and rolls of tape required for this 3-coat process.

Understanding Drywall Thickness

Drywall is sold in different thicknesses, each with specific uses:

  • 1/4 inch (6mm): Used for curved surfaces (like arches) or to cover old walls (skim coating). Very fragile.
  • 1/2 inch (12.5mm): The standard for residential walls. It is easy to cut and hang but can sag if used on ceilings with 24" O.C. joist spacing.
  • 5/8 inch (15mm): Used for ceilings (for sag resistance) and fire-rated walls (like between a garage and a house). It is heavier and requires more effort to install.

Global Standards: US vs. UK vs. Australia

If you are building internationally, you must know the standard sheet sizes.

USA: Sheets are typically 4 feet wide and 8, 10, or 12 feet long. Thicknesses are in inches (1/2", 5/8").

UK & Australia: Plasterboard is sold in metric sizes, most commonly 1200mm wide x 2400mm long (which is 2.88 square meters). Thickness is 12.5mm for standard walls and 15mm for fire-rated.

Our calculators allow you to toggle between Imperial (sq ft) and Metric (sq m) so you can work with whichever standard is local.

Specialty Drywall: Moisture & Fire Resistance

Not all drywall is created equal. In certain areas of the home, standard white drywall will fail. You must use specialized boards.

1. Moisture-Resistant Drywall (Greenboard)

In bathrooms and kitchens, standard drywall will absorb moisture and grow mold. Moisture-resistant drywall (often called "greenboard" due to its green paper face) has a wax-like additive in the gypsum core and a water-resistant paper.

Note: Even greenboard is not waterproof. In high-moisture areas like inside a shower, you must use cement board (backer board) or a waterproof membrane over the drywall. For calculating tile over cement board, use our Tile Calculator.

2. Fire-Rated Drywall (Type X)

Type X drywall has a special gypsum core with glass fibers that makes it highly fire-resistant. It is required by code in certain areas:

  • Between an attached garage and the living space.
  • On the ceiling of a basement (if there's living space above).
  • In multi-family dwellings (apartments) to separate units.

Type X is typically 5/8 inch thick and comes with a fire rating (e.g., 1-hour or 2-hour). Our Drywall Calculator supports both standard and 5/8" sheets.

The Art of Sanding and Priming

After the final coat of mud is dry, you must sand the walls. The goal is not to sand off the mud, but to feather the edges so they blend into the paper.

Use a pole sander with 120-grit or 150-grit paper. For the final pass, use a bright light angled against the wall to highlight any ridges or scratches. Once sanded, you must apply a PVA primer. Drywall paper is highly porous and will soak up paint unevenly if not primed. Priming ensures a smooth, even finish.

For paint quantity estimates, use our Paint Coverage Calculator.

Frequently Asked Questions

Drywall Calculator FAQs

Direct answers to common questions about drywall sheets, mud, and installation.

01

How many drywall sheets do I need for a room?
Calculate the total square footage of your walls and ceiling, subtract the area of doors and windows, and divide by the square footage of one sheet (a standard 4x8 sheet is 32 sq ft). Add 10% for waste. Use our Drywall Calculator for an exact count.
02

How much joint compound (mud) do I need per sheet?
As a general rule, you will use about 1 gallon of joint compound for every 100 square feet of drywall. This includes taping, filling, and the final skim coat. Use our Drywall Finishing Calculator for precise estimates.
03

What is the standard thickness of drywall?
The most common residential thickness is 1/2 inch. Use 5/8 inch for ceilings (for extra sag resistance) and fire-rated walls, and 1/4 inch for curved surfaces. In the UK and Australia, plasterboard is typically 12.5mm thick.
04

How many screws do I need per sheet of drywall?
A standard 4x8 sheet requires about 32 screws if studs are 16 inches on center, or 24 screws if studs are 24 inches on center. Always use coarse-thread screws for wood studs and fine-thread screws for metal studs.
05

Do I need to tape drywall joints?
Yes. Taping reinforces the joints and prevents them from cracking as the house settles. Apply joint compound, embed the tape, and then apply two or three thin coats of mud over it. Our Drywall Finishing Calculator estimates tape and mud.
06

How long does it take to install drywall?
A professional crew can hang and finish an average room in 2-3 days. The process includes hanging the sheets, taping, applying three coats of mud, sanding, and priming. DIY installations often take longer due to the learning curve.
07

What is the difference between plasterboard and drywall?
They are essentially the same thing. Drywall is the North American term, while plasterboard is used in the UK and Australia. Both consist of a gypsum core sandwiched between layers of paper. Our tools support both US and metric sizing.
