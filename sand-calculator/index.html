What Is a Sand Calculator?

A sand calculator is a tool that estimates how much sand you need for a project — measured in volume (cubic feet, cubic yards, cubic metres), weight (pounds, kilograms, tons, tonnes) or the number of standard bags. Instead of guessing and ending up with too little sand (which means a trip back to the supplier and delays in your project) or too much (which means wasted money sitting in your yard), you enter your project's dimensions and the calculator works out the exact quantity.

Sand quantity depends on several factors: the shape of the area you are filling (rectangular, circular or irregular), the dimensions, the depth of the sand layer, the type of sand you are using (dry, wet, packed or loose — each has a different density), and the waste allowance for spillage and compaction. This calculator takes all of these into account so you get a reliable estimate before you buy.

Sand is one of the most versatile building materials in the world. It is used in concrete mixes, mortar mixes, plaster, paver bases, sandboxes, garden beds, pool filters, playgrounds, golf courses and countless other applications. Each application requires a specific type and grade of sand, but the maths behind how much you need is the same. Whether you are filling a child's sandbox, preparing a paver base, or mixing concrete for a foundation, accurate sand estimation saves time, money and frustration.

Whether you are filling a sandbox in Texas, prepping a paver base in London, top-dressing a lawn in Sydney, or mixing concrete in Toronto, this calculator gives you an accurate estimate in both imperial and metric units.

How to Use This Sand Calculator

Using this sand calculator is straightforward. Follow these six steps:

  1. Choose the shape. Most projects use a rectangular or square area (sandbox, garden bed, paver base). For a round planter, fire pit or circular pool base, choose the circular option. If you already know the total square footage, choose the area-based option.
  2. Enter the dimensions. For a rectangular area, provide length, width and depth. For a circular area, provide diameter and depth. For an area-based calculation, provide total area and depth. Use the same units shown on each field.
  3. Select the sand type. Sand density varies by moisture content. Dry sand weighs about 100 lb per cubic foot (1,600 kg/m³). Wet sand weighs about 120 lb per cubic foot. Packed sand weighs 105 lb per cubic foot, and loose sand weighs 90 lb per cubic foot. Choose the closest match to your situation.
  4. Set a waste allowance. We default to 5% for smooth bases. Use 10% for standard conditions and 15% for rough or uneven bases. Sand also compacts by 10–15% when properly tamped, so it is better to slightly overestimate.
  5. Choose your unit system. Use the toggle at the top to switch between Imperial (feet, inches, pounds) and Metric (metres, centimetres, kilograms). Your preference is saved for next time.
  6. Click Calculate. The result shows volume in cubic feet, cubic yards, cubic metres, plus weight in pounds, kilograms, tons and tonnes — and the number of standard bags needed.

Pro Tip: Always order 5–10% extra sand beyond the calculated quantity. Sand can be spilled during transport, compacted more than expected, or wasted due to uneven subgrade. Having spares on hand means you can finish the project without a trip back to the supplier.

Formula Used by This Calculator

All calculations are performed internally in metric units, then converted back to your preferred unit system for display. The following formulas are used:

Step 1 — Rectangular Volume

Volume = Length × Width × Depth All measurements must be in the same unit.
Depth is usually given in inches or centimetres — convert to feet or metres first.

Step 2 — Circular Volume

Volume = π × r² × Depth Where r = radius (diameter ÷ 2).
π (pi) ≈ 3.14159.

Step 3 — Area-Based Volume

Volume = Area × Depth Use when you already know the total square footage or square metres.

Step 4 — Weight Calculation

Weight = Volume × Density Dry sand: 100 lb/ft³ (1,600 kg/m³)
Wet sand: 120 lb/ft³ (1,920 kg/m³)
Packed sand: 105 lb/ft³ (1,680 kg/m³)
Loose sand: 90 lb/ft³ (1,440 kg/m³)

Step 5 — Apply Waste Allowance

Final Volume = Volume × (1 + Waste% ÷ 100) Standard recommendation: 5–10%.

Step 6 — Bags of Sand

Bags = Weight ÷ Bag Weight 50 lb bag = 0.5 cu ft (0.014 m³)
25 kg bag = 0.0156 m³

Understanding the Variables

  • Volume: The total space the sand will occupy, in cubic units.
  • Depth: The thickness of the sand layer you are installing.
  • Density: The weight of sand per unit volume, which varies by moisture and compaction.
  • Waste %: Extra material for spillage, compaction and uneven subgrade.
  • Bag Weight: The weight of sand in one retail bag (50 lb or 25 kg standard).

Why Round Up, Not Down?

Sand calculations involve rounding in two places: volume and bag count. Always round up, never down. If your calculation gives 7.8 cubic feet, order 8. If it gives 15.4 bags, order 16. Running short mid-project is far more expensive than having spare sand at the end — especially if you have already opened the bags and cannot return them.

Sand Density and Compaction

One of the most overlooked aspects of sand estimation is compaction. Freshly poured sand is loose and contains voids. When properly tamped or vibrated, sand compacts by 10–15%. This means the volume you install will occupy less space than the volume you order. Always add a waste allowance for compaction, especially for paver bases and concrete mixes. Wet sand is also denser than dry sand, so the same volume weighs more. Our calculator lets you select the density type that best matches your situation.

Worked Example

Let us work through three examples — a child's sandbox, a paver base for a patio, and a garden bed top-dressing.

Example 1 — Child's Sandbox (4 ft × 4 ft, 6 in deep, dry sand, 10% waste)

  1. Convert depth to feet: 6 ÷ 12 = 0.5 ft
  2. Volume: 4 × 4 × 0.5 = 8 cu ft
  3. Apply 10% waste: 8 × 1.10 = 8.8 cu ft
  4. Weight (dry sand, 100 lb/ft³): 8.8 × 100 = 880 lb
  5. Bags (50 lb each): 880 ÷ 50 = 17.6 → 18 bags

Result: You need about 8.8 cubic feet of sand, weighing approximately 880 lb — or 18 bags of 50 lb play sand.

Example 2 — Paver Base (12 ft × 15 ft, 1 in deep, packed sand, 10% waste)

  1. Convert depth to feet: 1 ÷ 12 = 0.083 ft
  2. Volume: 12 × 15 × 0.083 = 14.94 cu ft
  3. Apply 10% waste: 14.94 × 1.10 = 16.43 cu ft
  4. Weight (packed sand, 105 lb/ft³): 16.43 × 105 = 1,725 lb
  5. Cubic yards: 16.43 ÷ 27 = 0.61 cu yd
  6. Bags (50 lb each): 1,725 ÷ 50 = 34.5 → 35 bags

Result: You need about 16.4 cubic feet of paver sand (0.61 cubic yards), weighing approximately 1,725 lb — or 35 bags of 50 lb. For a larger project, order 0.75 cubic yards of bulk paver sand.

Example 3 — Circular Fire Pit Base (3 m diameter, 10 cm deep, dry sand, 5% waste)

  1. Radius: 3 ÷ 2 = 1.5 m
  2. Depth in metres: 10 ÷ 100 = 0.1 m
  3. Volume: π × 1.5² × 0.1 = 0.707 m³
  4. Apply 5% waste: 0.707 × 1.05 = 0.742 m³
  5. Weight (dry sand, 1,600 kg/m³): 0.742 × 1,600 = 1,187 kg
  6. Bags (25 kg each): 1,187 ÷ 25 = 47.5 → 48 bags

Result: You need about 0.74 cubic metres of sand, weighing approximately 1,187 kg — or 48 bags of 25 kg.

Pro Tip: For paver bases, always use sharp sand (also called concrete sand or grit sand), not builder's sand or play sand. Sharp sand compacts firmly and provides a stable base, while soft sand shifts and settles over time. For sandboxes, use washed play sand that is free of silica dust and contaminants.

Types of Sand

Not all sand is the same. Different types are designed for different applications:

  • Sharp Sand (Concrete Sand): Coarse, angular grains. Used for concrete mixes, paver bases and floor screeds. Compacts firmly and drains well.
  • Builder's Sand (Soft Sand): Fine, rounded grains. Used for bricklaying mortar, plaster and general construction. Not suitable for paver bases.
  • Play Sand: Washed, screened, fine-grained. Used for sandboxes and playgrounds. Free of silica dust and contaminants.
  • Paver Sand (Polymeric Sand): Fine sand mixed with polymers. Swept into paver joints, then activated with water to form a hard, weed-resistant joint.
  • Masonry Sand: Fine, clean sand. Used for mortar, stucco and plaster. Also called "fat sand".
  • Fill Sand: Unscreened, all-purpose sand. Used for filling holes, leveling ground and backfill. Cheapest option.
  • Silica Sand: High quartz content. Used for pool filters, sandblasting and glass-making. Not for construction.
  • Pit Sand: Coarse, naturally occurring sand. Used for concrete, masonry and plaster. Contains some clay and silt.
  • River Sand: Fine, smooth sand from riverbeds. Used for plaster, masonry and concrete in some regions.

Industry Standards and Codes

Sand used in construction must comply with several industry standards and specifications:

  • ASTM C33: Standard Specification for Concrete Aggregates (USA)
  • ASTM C144: Standard Specification for Aggregate for Masonry Mortar (USA)
  • ASTM D1073: Standard Specification for Fine Aggregate for Bituminous Paving Mixtures
  • BS EN 12620: Aggregates for Concrete (UK / Europe)
  • BS EN 13139: Aggregates for Mortar (UK / Europe)
  • IS 383: Specification for Coarse and Fine Aggregates from Natural Sources for Concrete (India)
  • AS 2758.1: Aggregates and Rock for Engineering Purposes (Australia)
  • ASTM C136: Standard Test Method for Sieve Analysis of Fine and Coarse Aggregates

Always check your local building codes before using sand in structural applications. Sand quality directly affects concrete strength and mortar bond — using the wrong grade can cause structural failure.

Tools and Equipment Needed

To work with sand efficiently, you will need the following tools:

  • Essential: Shovel, wheelbarrow, rake, tamper (hand tamper or plate compactor), level, tape measure, garden hose (for settling)
  • Recommended: Screed board (for leveling paver sand), screed rails, landscape fabric (for weed barrier under sand), edging
  • Safety: Dust mask (silica dust is harmful), safety glasses, gloves, steel-toe boots, sun protection
  • Optional: Plate compactor (for large paver bases), mechanical spreader (for accurate application), laser level

Safety first: sand dust contains silica, which is harmful to lungs. Always wear a dust mask when handling dry sand, especially in enclosed spaces. Use a wetting agent (garden hose) to suppress dust during spreading.

Maintenance and Care

Proper maintenance ensures sand performs as intended:

  • Keep sand dry: Wet sand is heavier and prone to contamination. Store bags in a dry, covered location.
  • Prevent contamination: Cover bulk sand piles with tarps to prevent leaves, dirt and debris from mixing in.
  • Re-level periodically: Paver sand and sandbox sand settle over time. Rake and re-level every 1–2 years.
  • Replace play sand: Sandbox play sand should be replaced every 2–3 years to prevent bacteria buildup.
  • Prevent weed growth: Use landscape fabric under paver sand to prevent weeds from pushing through.
  • Check drainage: Sand is porous, but if water pools, the base may need to be regraded.
  • Watch for erosion: Sand in garden beds and paver joints can erode. Add more sand as needed.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Even experienced contractors make mistakes when calculating sand quantities. Here are the most common ones:

  • Forgetting compaction: Sand compacts by 10–15% when tamped. Always add waste allowance for compaction.
  • Using the wrong sand type: Sharp sand for pavers, builder's sand for mortar, play sand for sandboxes. Wrong type = poor results.
  • Ignoring density differences: Wet sand weighs 20% more than dry sand. Density affects weight but not volume.
  • Not leveling the base: Sand must be screeded flat before installing pavers. Uneven base causes paver movement.
  • Applying sand too thick or too thin: Paver base should be 1 inch. Too thin doesn't level; too thick settles unevenly.
  • Not using landscape fabric: Weeds grow through sand without fabric barrier. Fabric prevents this.
  • Underestimating waste: Always add 5–10% for spillage and uneven subgrade.
  • Rounding down: Always round up — never down — when ordering sand.
  • Mixing metric and imperial: Keep all measurements in the same unit system throughout. Our calculator handles conversions automatically.
  • Ignoring safety: Silica dust is harmful. Always wear a dust mask when working with dry sand.
