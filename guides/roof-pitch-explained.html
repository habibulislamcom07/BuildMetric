Roof Pitch Explained: Why 6:12 and 26.57° Are the Same Roof

Confused by rise-over-run versus degrees? Learn exactly how to convert roof pitch, calculate slope percentage, and avoid the costly material errors that happen on site every day.

If you ask three tradespeople on a UK site what the roof pitch is, you might get "30 degrees," "7 in 12," and "about 58 percent." All three could be describing the exact same roof — but if any one of them is using the wrong conversion factor, your material order will be short or your rafters will be cut wrong.

Roof pitch is a mathematical statement about a triangle, not a stylistic preference. In construction, that triangle governs how much material you buy, whether a specific membrane is permitted under local code, and how the structure resists wind uplift.

01. The 3 Ways to Describe a Roof

The confusion happens because different regions (and different trades) use different ways to express the same geometry. There are three primary methods:

Ratio

Rise : Run

The US Standard (e.g., 6:12)

Angle

Degrees (°)

The UK/AU Standard (e.g., 26.57°)

Slope

Percentage (%)

Used for drainage (e.g., 50%)

The Rise-Over-Run Ratio

In the United States, pitch is almost always expressed as inches of rise per 12 inches of horizontal run. A "6:12" pitch (often spoken as "six-twelve") means the roof rises 6 inches for every 12 inches it travels horizontally. This is the easiest method for a framer to use on site, as it directly translates to the cuts on a framing square.

The Angle in Degrees

In the UK, Australia, and most of Europe, roof pitch is specified in degrees. A 30° roof is a 30° roof regardless of whether you measure in metric or imperial. This is the standard on architectural drawings because it integrates seamlessly with structural engineering software and global standards like BS 5534 and Eurocode 5.

Slope Percentage

Slope percentage is most commonly used for drainage calculations, flat roofs, and civil engineering. It is the rise divided by the run, multiplied by 100. A 6:12 pitch has a 50% slope. Crucially, a 50% slope is not a 50° angle. It is only 26.57°. Confusing the two is a common and expensive estimating error.

02. The Trigonometry (How to Calculate It)

If you need to convert between these three formats without a calculator, you need basic trigonometry. The roof forms a right-angled triangle where the run is the adjacent side, the rise is the opposite side, and the rafter is the hypotenuse.

The Golden Formulas:

  • Angle (θ) = arctan(Rise / Run)
  • Slope % = (Rise / Run) × 100
  • Rafter Length = Run / cos(θ) (or use the pitch multiplier)

Let's use a standard 6:12 pitch as an example:

  1. Calculate the ratio: 6 ÷ 12 = 0.5
  2. Calculate the angle: arctan(0.5) = 26.565°
  3. Calculate the slope: 0.5 × 100 = 50%

If you have a 26.565° angle from a UK drawing and need to cut rafters on a US site, you convert back using tan(26.565°) = 0.5. Multiply by 12, and you get "6 in 12".

03. Why the Difference Costs Money

The most expensive mistake in roofing estimating is confusing slope percentage with the rafter length multiplier (also called the secant).

If you have a 6:12 pitch, the slope is 50%. A common error is to take the flat footprint area of the roof and add 50% to account for the pitch. This is wrong, and it will cost you thousands in wasted material.

The Golden Rule of Roof Area

To find the true surface area of a pitched roof, you multiply the flat footprint by the Rafter Multiplier — NOT the slope percentage.

Footprint × 1.50

Wrong (Using 50% slope)

Footprint × 1.118

Correct (Using true multiplier)

For a 6:12 pitch, the rafter multiplier is 1.118 (which is \(1 / \cos(26.57°)\)). If your flat footprint is 3,000 sq ft, the actual roof surface area is 3,354 sq ft.

If you had ordered based on the 50% slope error (3,000 × 1.5), you would have ordered 4,500 sq ft of shingles. That is an over-order of over 1,100 square feet — enough to cover an entire extra small garage, and a massive hit to your material budget.

04. Building Codes: IRC vs BS 5534

Pitch is not just a design choice; it is heavily regulated because it dictates how water sheds and how wind uplift is managed.

United States (IRC 2021)

Under the International Residential Code (Section R905), asphalt shingles generally require a minimum slope of 2:12 (about 9.46°). However, at that low pitch, the underlayment requirements become much stricter — often requiring a double layer of underlayment or a self-adhering ice-and-water shield covering the entire roof deck. Below 2:12, standard shingles are not permitted, and you must switch to a flat-roof membrane (like EPDM or TPO).

United Kingdom (BS 5534)

In the UK, minimum pitch is dictated by BS 5534 and the manufacturer's installation manual for the specific tile or slate. A standard clay plain tile typically requires a minimum pitch of 35°. However, this minimum can be lowered (sometimes to 15°) if you install a specific breathable underlay and increase the headlap of the tiles. Wind-driven rain exposure (calculated by location) also plays a massive role here.

Australia (AS 1684 & NCC)

In Australia, while pitch affects drainage, the most critical code factor is wind classification (AS 4055). Low-pitch roofs in cyclonic regions (like Far North Queensland) require significantly stronger tie-downs and fasteners to prevent the roof from being sucked off during a storm.

The Bottom Line

Roof pitch is a simple triangle, but the difference between communicating it in degrees, rise-over-run, or slope percentage causes real-world estimating errors. Always confirm whether a figure is a ratio, an angle, or a multiplier before you order material or set up your saw.

Frequently Asked Questions

Roof Pitch FAQs

01 How do you convert a 6:12 pitch to degrees?

Divide the rise by the run (6 ÷ 12 = 0.5). Then, calculate the inverse tangent (arctan) of 0.5, which equals 26.565°. This means a roof that rises 6 inches for every 12 inches of run has an angle of approximately 26.57 degrees.

02 What is the difference between roof pitch and roof slope?

Roof pitch is typically expressed as a ratio of rise-over-run (e.g., 6:12), primarily used in the US. Roof slope is often expressed as a percentage (e.g., 50%) or an angle in degrees (e.g., 26.57°), used in the UK, Australia, and civil engineering. They describe the same geometry but are not interchangeable mathematically; a 50% slope is not the same as a 50° angle.

03 What is the minimum roof pitch for asphalt shingles?

Under the International Residential Code (IRC 2021), the minimum pitch for standard asphalt shingles is 2:12 (about 9.46°). However, roofs between 2:12 and 4:12 require a double layer of underlayment to prevent water infiltration. Below 2:12, standard shingles cannot be used, and a flat-roof membrane is required.

04 What is the roof pitch multiplier?

The roof pitch multiplier (or rafter multiplier) is a number used to calculate the true surface area of a pitched roof. It is equal to the secant of the roof angle (1 / cos θ). To find your actual roof area, multiply the flat footprint area by this multiplier. For example, a 6:12 pitch has a multiplier of 1.118.

05 How do I calculate rafter length from pitch?

To calculate rafter length, you can use the Pythagorean theorem (a² + b² = c²) where 'a' is the rise and 'b' is the run. Alternatively, divide the run by the cosine of the pitch angle. For a 6:12 pitch, the multiplier is 1.118, so a 12-foot run would require a rafter length of 12 × 1.118 = 13.416 feet (before adding for overhang).

06 Does a steeper roof pitch cost more?

Yes, generally. First, the actual surface area of the roof increases, requiring more materials. Second, steeper roofs are more difficult and dangerous to walk on, increasing labor costs and often requiring additional safety equipment like roof jacks or harnesses.

07 What is a walkable roof pitch?

A standard roof is generally considered walkable without special fall protection up to a 6:12 pitch (26.57°). Pitches steeper than 6:12 are typically considered too dangerous to walk on without roof jacks, harnesses, or scaffolding. A 4:12 pitch is generally comfortable to walk on.
