Quick Answer: How Many Joist Hangers Do I Need?

You need exactly one joist hanger for every standard joist where it connects to a ledger board, rim joist, or beam. For a typical deck with joists spaced 16 inches on center (O.C.), you will need roughly 1 hanger per 16 inches of ledger length, plus a few extra for double joists or stair stringers. Our calculator above factors in the length of your ledger, spacing, and application type to give you the exact Bill of Materials (BOM).

The Structural Role of Joist Hangers

In deck and floor construction, joist hangers are non-negotiable. A joist hanger is a metal bracket (typically galvanized steel) designed to support the end of a wooden joist, transferring its load to a ledger board or beam. Without proper hangers, joists rely entirely on toe-nailing (driving nails at an angle through the joist into the ledger), which is structurally insufficient and a leading cause of deck collapses.

Engineers categorize forces into two main types: gravity (downward load) and shear (lateral force). While the decking and joists handle the gravity, the joist hangers are critical for resisting the shear forces—the tendency of the joist to pull away from the house or beam.

Building codes across the globe recognize this. In the US, the International Residential Code (IRC) R502.6 explicitly requires that joists framing into the side of a wood girder or ledger must be supported by approved metal hangers. In the UK, the Eurocode 5 (BS EN 1995) and BS 5268 outline strict requirements for joist connections to prevent differential movement. Similarly, in Australia, AS 1684 (Residential timber-framed construction) mandates the use of proprietary metal hangers for framing into ledgers.

Using our calculator ensures you order the precise number of hangers, eliminating the risk of running out mid-project (which forces a dangerous work stoppage) or over-ordering expensive hardware.

Face-Mount vs. Top-Flange Hangers

When selecting hangers, you must choose between two primary styles. The choice depends on how your ledger or beam is structured:

  • Face-Mount Hangers: These are the most common type for retrofits and standard decks. They attach to the face of the ledger or rim board. They require a specific depth of wood (e.g., a 2x8 joist needs a hanger designed for a 2x8). Our calculator defaults to this type.
  • Top-Flange Hangers: These are used when joists sit flush with the top of a beam (flush beam framing). The hanger wraps over the top of the beam, providing superior load capacity and making the installation of the joist much easier.

For double joists (often used at the edge of a deck or where a stair stringer attaches), you must use a double joist hanger or two standard hangers side-by-side. Our tool automatically adjusts the quantity and suggests the correct hardware based on your selection.

Global Fastener Standards: Nails vs. Screws

The strongest joist hanger in the world is useless if it's installed with the wrong fasteners. Manufacturer specifications (like Simpson Strong-Tie) and building codes dictate the exact type, length, and quantity of fasteners required.

Contrary to popular belief among DIYers, standard deck screws or drywall screws should never be used in structural joist hangers. They have low shear strength and will snap under load. You must use either hot-dip galvanized joist hanger nails or structural screws (like Simpson Strong-Tie SD screws) that are specifically rated for hangers.

How to Calculate Hanger Spacing and Quantity

The math to find the total number of joist hangers is relatively straightforward, provided you know your joist spacing.

The Formula

Number of Hangers = (Ledger Length in inches ÷ Joist Spacing in inches) + 1

Example: If your deck ledger is 16 feet long (192 inches) and your joists are spaced 16 inches on center:
192 ÷ 16 = 12 spaces.
You need 12 hangers for the spaces, plus 1 for the end joist. So, you need 13 standard joist hangers.

Installation Best Practices: How to Nail a Joist Hanger

Installing a joist hanger seems simple—you just hammer in some nails, right? Wrong. A staggering number of deck failures are traced back to improperly installed joist hangers. To ensure your framing meets code and can handle the structural loads, follow these strict engineering guidelines:

1. Fill Every Single Hole

Manufactured hangers (like those from Simpson Strong-Tie) are tested with specific fastener patterns. If a hanger has 10 holes, you must fill all 10. Some hangers have "positive placement" holes for a special metal-connector nail gun, and standard round holes for hand-nailing. Do not skip holes just because they are hard to reach. Each nail contributes to the hanger's published load capacity.

2. Do Not Overdrive the Nails

When using a nail gun (pneumatic or gas-powered), it is easy to drive the nail too deep. If the nail head sinks into the steel of the hanger or the wood, the connection loses a significant portion of its shear strength. The nail head must sit flush against the metal. If you overdrive a nail, you must pull it out and replace it with a new one in a different hole (or a slightly larger nail if the hole is stripped).

3. The "10d x 1.5-inch" Rule

For standard 2x lumber (which is actually 1.5 inches thick), you must use nails that are long enough to penetrate the ledger board by at least 1.5 inches, but not so long that they poke out the other side. In the US, standard 10d x 1-1/2" joist hanger nails are specifically designed for this. Never substitute these with 16d framing nails, as the thicker shank can split the wood, and the shorter length provides inadequate grip.

Galvanic Corrosion: Why Metal Matters

When two dissimilar metals come into contact in the presence of an electrolyte (like moisture), a chemical reaction called galvanic corrosion occurs. The weaker metal corrodes rapidly, turning to dust and destroying the structural connection.

This is a massive issue in modern deck building because today's pressure-treated lumber (especially in the US and Australia) is treated with ACQ (Alkaline Copper Quaternary) or Copper Azole, which are highly corrosive to standard galvanized steel.

The Solution: You must match your hardware to your lumber.

  • If using ACQ-treated lumber, you must use Hot-Dip Galvanized (HDG) hangers and fasteners, or stainless steel.
  • Standard electro-galvanized (G90) hangers will rust away in a few years if used with ACQ wood.
  • If you are building near saltwater (coastal areas), Stainless Steel 316 is mandatory for both the hanger and the nails, regardless of the wood treatment.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Hanging off a Cantilevered Ledger: Joist hangers transfer load downward. If your ledger board is not properly flashed or bolted to the house's rim joist with lag screws or structural through-bolts, the hangers will just pull the ledger off the house. Always ensure the ledger is structurally sound.

2. Using Deck Screws: Deck screws are brittle and have very low shear strength. They will snap under the weight of a loaded deck. Always use the manufacturer-specified structural nails or structural screws (like Simpson SD screws).

3. Reusing Old Hangers: If you are renovating a deck and pull off an old hanger, do not reuse it. The nail holes will be stripped, the metal may be fatigued, and the galvanization may be compromised. Always buy new hardware.

Build a Deck You Can Trust

Joist hangers are the invisible heroes of your deck's structure. Getting the quantity right, buying the correct galvanized models, and nailing them correctly is the difference between a deck that lasts 30 years and one that becomes a safety hazard.

Frequently Asked Questions

Joist Hanger FAQs

Direct answers to common questions about hardware, nails, and installation.

01

How many joist hangers do I need per square foot?
You generally need one hanger for every joist connecting to the ledger. For a deck with joists spaced 16 inches on center (O.C.), this equates to about 0.75 to 1 hanger per linear foot of the ledger board. To get the exact count for your specific deck length and spacing, use the calculator above.
02

What kind of nails should I use for joist hangers?
You must use the manufacturer's specified fasteners. In the US, this is typically 10d x 1-1/2 inch galvanized joist hanger nails. Alternatively, you can use structural screws (like Simpson Strong-Tie SD screws) rated for hangers. Never use standard deck screws or drywall screws, as they lack the shear strength required for structural connections.
03

Can I use deck screws instead of nails for joist hangers?
No. Standard deck screws are too brittle and have low shear strength. Under the load of a deck, they can snap, causing the joist to pull away from the ledger. If you prefer screws over nails, you must use specifically engineered structural screws (like Simpson SD screws) that have been tested and approved for use in joist hangers.
04

What is the difference between face-mount and top-flange hangers?
Face-mount hangers attach to the vertical face of a ledger board or rim joist. They are the most common type for standard deck framing. Top-flange hangers wrap over the top of a beam and are used when the joists need to be flush with the top of the beam. Our calculator defaults to standard face-mount sizing, but you can check our framing calculator for more complex layouts.
05

What size hanger do I need for a 2x10 joist?
For a nominal 2x10 joist, you need a hanger designed specifically for a 2x10. In the US, this is typically a model like the Simpson Strong-Tie LUS210. A hanger for a 2x8 (like the LUS28) will not fit, and a 2x12 hanger will be too loose. Always match the hanger model exactly to your joist size.
06

Why are my joist hangers rusting so fast?
You are likely experiencing galvanic corrosion. Modern pressure-treated lumber (like ACQ) contains high levels of copper, which is highly corrosive to standard galvanized steel. If you use standard G90 hangers with ACQ wood, they will rust rapidly. You must use Hot-Dip Galvanized (HDG) or stainless steel hangers and nails when working with modern treated lumber.
07

Do I need joist hangers on both sides of a ledger?
Usually, hangers are only required where the joist connects to the ledger. The other end of the joist typically rests in a beam or on a support post. However, if the joist spans between two ledgers, or if a double joist is required for structural support, you will need hangers on both ends.
